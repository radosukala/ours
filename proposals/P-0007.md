## The problem

Three months of work has not put anything in front of people that they
would use without being asked. The rounds between 31 August and 24
September kept finding the same gap. Every earlier entrance had one of
these faults:

- it was a mechanism: a gate, a register, a vote on a button's colour;
- it was a single group's tool, which needed a group to be chosen first;
- it was a contract between a club and a builder, which builders would not
  take.

None of them was the asset the thesis itself names. That asset is the
network: who is connected to whom.

The fact-checks establish four points:

- **The large networks stopped being about friends, by their own
  account.** In January 2025, US users spent 17% of their Facebook time and
  7% of their Instagram time on friends' content. Those figures come from
  Meta's trial exhibits in *FTC v. Meta*. Meta argued there that people now
  mostly watch unconnected videos, and it won.
- **The promise of "friends, in order, no ads" has been made before.** It
  was heard, often loudly, and then it broke. Some networks faded after a
  spike. Others kept their promise until a sale: Ello (sold, and nine years
  of posts later lost), BeReal (sold, then asked EU users daily to accept ad
  tracking), Path (sold, then closed).
- **The friends graph is the most dangerous dataset there is.** About
  270,000 app installs exposed up to 87 million people through their
  friends.
- **Private posts and private connections are not yet available on the
  open alternatives.** AT Protocol's "Spaces" is in a developer alpha as of
  20 August 2026.

## Affected

- Everyone who would connect.
- The founder, who funds and operates it at launch.
- Builders, who are not admitted to the graph in the first version.

## The proposal

1. **Start with the connections.** OURS's first product is a home for
   friends (mutual) and the people you choose to follow (one-way). It
   shows their posts in order, with an end when you are caught up. The
   first act is a person inviting another: *Connect with me on OURS.*
2. **The difference is who can change it.** It is not "no algorithm", which
   others already offer. At launch the honest line is *founder-led and
   founder-funded; working toward control by the people using it*. "Not for
   sale" is not said in the present tense: having no investors does not
   stop a founder selling, and a public benefit corporation does not by
   itself give users control.
3. **Transparent from the first day,** in three parts:
   - **code:** open source, and the running version identified;
   - **money:** a public ledger with contributions received, spending,
     what remains, and what is coming. Commitments are shown as
     commitments and estimates as estimates;
   - **power:** a page showing who controls the domain, infrastructure,
     releases, moderation and funds.
4. **Safety through simplicity.**
   - Store the minimum: accepted connections, follows, posts, replies,
     likes, blocks and reports.
   - No contact upload, no search, no "people you may know".
   - No third-party code touches the graph.
   - Later applications receive abilities, never lists.
5. **Day two and the surge.**
   - The feed has an end.
   - One weekly email says who posted, not what.
   - Growth is by invitation only, with a limit per person.
6. **The address is `our.one`**, as `D-0004` assigned to products. Mobile
   comes as web first, then iOS, then Android, sharing one core. Reporting
   and blocking are in the foundation.

- **The ledger's first recorded entries:** the founder's contribution once
