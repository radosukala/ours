## The problem

The front page built under `M-0013` says what our.one is for a visitor:
just your people, newest first, and an end. The review, written for the
founder on 30 September 2026, found what it still lacks:

1. **The everyday benefit never meets the reason the project exists.** The
   page sells relief from scrolling, and the members' say comes only as a
   promise to hand things over.
2. **The page doesn't say what our.one is.** Nothing calls it a social
   network.
3. **"Get in" is vague.** The form only ever joins, or joins the waiting
   list: an address that already has an account gets no email from it.
4. **On a phone, the product comes last.** A visitor reads the form and the
   handover before the picture of the feed.
5. **Four lines say more than the facts, or less clearly than they could:**
   - *"Ads came to WhatsApp."*: Meta announced ads in Status, in the
     Updates tab, away from personal chats, rolling out over months;
   - *"held by that contract, not yet by law"*: confusing to a visitor;
   - *"leave with everything"*: can read as moving the whole network
     elsewhere, where the export is a file of your own data;
   - the card hands over "it", all of our.one, where the contract names
     three things (raised by the verifiers of `M-0013`, for the founder).
6. **The footer's status line** still says "Handed to its members", the
   wording the card dropped (raised by the same verifiers).
7. **The invitation is impersonal.** The last words are "Bring your
   people.", and the answer about friends not being there is "That's what
   your 10 invites are for."

## What is proposed

The agent checked each of the review's points against the code, the
contract and the sources, and built a prototype on the page as it is
(`docs-internal/prototype/front-v4.html`, not published). It proposed:

1. **The lede says what it is:** "A social network for your friends and the
   people you choose to follow." Following exists in the app.
2. **Join:** the button says "Join our.one", or "Join the waiting list" when
   no seat is open.
3. **On a phone, the picture comes before the card.**
4. **The card names the three things and says who holds them today.**
5. **The reason, in the founder's voice,** opening the section on who runs
   our.one: *"The people make the network."*
6. **The four lines say exactly what is true:** what Meta announced, linked
   to its own words; who holds what today; what the export holds.
7. **A personal invitation:** a first step in the answer about friends, and
   the page ends on *"Who would you like to hear from?"*
8. **The invite page** carries the same lede, says it is free, and links to
   the promise.
9. **The footer's status line** in line with the card.

**Not proposed, from the review:**

- **Its headline,** *"Your people are the point."*
  - "Just your people. Then you're done." names both things that make
    our.one different, and the review's names neither.
  - It goes into the five-stranger test instead, which the review itself
    asks for.
- **A mission line and a line of trust in place of the signed card** on the
  first screen. A specific promise signed by a named person carries more
  than "a network the people using it can control". The card was fixed
  instead.
- **A block inviting builders to help.**
  - Nothing yet says how an outsider's change gets in.
  - `foundation/SHARED-TOOL.md` §9 says builders are paid contributors
    under clear agreements, and that "a home for unlimited volunteer
    software is a failure mode".
  - It needs its own decision.
- **Moving "Where did your friends go?" after the mission.** It is what
  makes a stranger want the product.

## Risks

- **The evidence is two readers' judgment,** the review's and an agent's.
  The five-stranger test is still owed before release.
- **The page grows by a paragraph.** It is placed below the first screen.
- **The footer's line is longer.** It says three things where it said one,
  because one was not exact.
