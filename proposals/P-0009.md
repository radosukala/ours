# P-0009 — The front door: the front page leads with the product

**Status:** ADOPTED, by the founder's decision of 29 September 2026,
recorded in [`D-0015`](../decisions/D-0015.md).
**Requests:** D-0015 (CONSTITUTIONAL). It amends `D-0012` §D.
**Proposed by:** an agent, answering the founder's question
**Drafted by:** the same agent
**Evidence:**
[`P-0009.evidence-conversation-2026-09-29.md`](./P-0009.evidence-conversation-2026-09-29.md)
(the founder's words, verbatim)

## The problem

The founder asked whether a stranger who had never heard of our.one would
join from the front page built under `M-0011`. The agent walked the whole
path a stranger takes on the local build: the front page, an email, the
link, the join form, the first screen.

**What it found.** The page answers *who owns it, and why trust him* before
*what is it, and why would I want it*. Trust is the second question, and
nobody asks it about something they don't want yet. In detail:

1. **The headline is about the founder.** *"Today it's mine"* are the first
   words a visitor reads. "It" is never defined, and the "I" has no face.
2. **The product is one sentence of mechanisms.** "In order", "with an end"
   and "No ranking" describe how it works, not why anyone would want it.
3. **The numbers read as an empty room** when shown beside the button:
   "11 people are in", "999 seats open" and "100,000". The count is a rank
   that means *early* in the handover story, and *empty* next to "Get in".
4. **The design is X's:** its palette, and a timeline with @handles.
5. **Nothing says the feed starts empty.** The joiner learns it on the
   first screen: *"Your feed is quiet. Invite someone you know."*

Everything below the first screen was also about trust.

## What is proposed

**The order: want, then how, then trust, then act.** The substance of
`D-0012` stays: the maintainer, the contract, the handover promise and the
status line.

1. **The first screen.**
   - The headline *"Just your people. Then you're done."*
   - A plain lede: posts from the people you choose, newest first, no ads
     and no suggested posts, and it tells you when you've seen them all.
   - Get in, with "Free to join. You get 10 invites to bring your people."
   - The promise, signed, as a card: *"I'll never sell our.one. When
     [threshold] people have joined, I hand it to its members, and they can
     replace me."*
   - A phone whose feed ends at *You're caught up*.
2. **"Where did your friends go?"** The court's finding that Americans
   spent 7% of their Instagram time on content from friends (FTC v. Meta,
   from Meta's January 2025 figures). Beside it, a ranked feed next to one
   that ends.
3. **How it works:** get in, bring your people, catch up and close it.
4. **"Keep your people. Change who runs it."**
   - WhatsApp in three dated lines.
   - The owner and maintainer line.
   - The handover promise in its listed words, and what holds it today.
   - The count, as a rank.
5. **Fair questions,** the product's first (free? my friends? an app?), then
   the three about trust kept from today.

**Not proposed:**

- the answer after Get in stays as it is (see `D-0015` §H);
- the invite page, a day-one invite step and photos are left for later
  decisions.

## Risks

- **The only evidence is one reader's judgment, and an AI's.** The
  five-stranger test is still owed; it should run before release.
- **A product-first headline may read like every friends-only app** that
  came before. The promise sits on the first screen for that reason.
- **The 7% figure is a claim with a number.** A human must confirm its
  source before it is published (AGENTS.md §10).
