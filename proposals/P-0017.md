# P-0017 — The first screen, for people who come from a post

**Status:** ADOPTED, by the founder's "ok go" of 5 October 2026, as recorded
in [`D-0024`](../decisions/D-0024.md).
**Requests:** D-0024 (CONSTITUTIONAL)
**Proposed by:** the founder
**Drafted by:** an agent, from the founder's words and what it measured on
the live site
**Evidence:** [`P-0017.evidence-conversation-2026-10-05.md`](./P-0017.evidence-conversation-2026-10-05.md)

## The problem

The founder is about to send people to our.one from a video and a post.
The video says three things in about a minute: what our.one is, what is
promised with a number, and what to do. The front door doesn't say those
three things first.

- **Too much, in the wrong order.** 1,435 words in six sections. The
  handover promise, the one concrete thing our.one offers, sits two
  sections down, beside the feed, with the count. The first screen says
  the mission and the status, and a lede that describes a mechanism
  ("bringing people and builders together to create services their users
  can control").
- **Twelve asks, one working door.** A visitor is asked to click twelve
  different things. Of the two the video provokes, "put me on the list"
  works (the waiting list, oldest first) and "here's the app I'd take
  back" is a dead end: *Draft a need* ends with a draft to copy, because
  the proposals address isn't set.
- **A count that can't move.** No seat is open. A counter beside a closed
  door is worse than no counter.
- **No link card.** X's crawler gets no `og:` or `twitter:` tags from
  our.one, so a link shows whatever X last cached for the domain, which was
  another site.

## What is proposed

1. **The first screen does the whole job,** in this order: the headline;
   one line of what it is; the promise, with the count against its
   threshold; the status line; one form. Everything below stays for people
   who read.
2. **One form, two fields:** the address, and an optional line, *"Which app
   would you take back?"* One button, with the words D-0016 §B already
   gives it. *I want to build* stays as a quiet second link.
3. **A need named is kept:** without the address, counted in public, read
   by the founder, not published. The privacy notice says so.
4. **The count, honestly:** accounts, as the contract counts, as a number
   against 100,000, never a bar; on the first screen and in the panel
   beside a member's feed.
5. **A link card:** title, description and an image in the identity, for
   X and the others.
6. **Two founder actions, no code:** a first wave of seats opened when the
   post goes out, and `PROPOSALS_EMAIL` set, so the existing drafts have a
   destination meanwhile.

## Alternatives considered

- **A progress bar.** At 1 of 100,000 it is an empty bar. The number is the
  story; the bar would hide it.
- **Needs linked to the address,** so the founder can reply. Rejected for
  now: it makes the free text personal data twice over and invites a
  conversation the site can't yet hold. Unlinked and counted is enough to
  show demand, and the post's replies carry the conversation.
- **Publishing the needs as a list.** The strongest version of the idea,
  and a moderation burden on one person. Left to a later decision, with
  the data kept so it can be made.
- **Cutting the long page.** Rejected: the people who read 1,400 words are
  the ones who build or join for the right reasons, and much of that
  wording is verified and quoted by the contract.

## Cost and risk

A new first screen, a counter, a `needs` table with a privacy line, and a
card: about a day of agent work, `ESTIMATED`, plus the verification
pattern. Reversible, except that needs named before a rollback would be
lost unless exported. The first screen's words are untested with
strangers; the revised message test (D-0020 §C) remains due before the
public launch.

## Unresolved

- the size of the first wave of seats;
- whether the needs are published later, and how they are moderated;
- how long a need is kept (the draft says 12 months);
- the message test with people who haven't met the project.
