## The problem

`D-0011` built a home for friends and the people you follow, and adopted
careful copy: *"Stay connected. On our terms."* The founder judged it not
credible and not bold enough.

- **"A feed without an algorithm" has been tried many times.** Ello, Vero,
  Path and BeReal were each heard, then faded or were sold.
- **The difference he sees:** a network run by a *maintainer* instead of an
  owner, under a public contract, with ownership passing to its people.
- **Five earlier launches found no users.** So he wants to prove the idea
  resonates before spending on legal structure, and to execute the transfer
  at a membership threshold.

## What is proposed

1. **A maintainer, not an owner.**
   - The founder, through his company, is our.one's *maintainer*.
   - The maintainer is bound by a public contract, which is also the terms
     people join under.
   - The contract lists what the maintainer cannot do. Each promise says
     what holds it today: the contract, the code or the law.
2. **The handover at a membership threshold.** This proposal proposes
   100,000; the founder offered "100k or 1M", and the choice is his
   `[CONFIRM]`.
   - When that many verified people are members, three things pass to a
     not-for-profit body of its members, founded by their vote under rules
     published beforehand:
     - the domain;
     - the database;
     - the right to replace the maintainer.
   - Until then the maintainer holds them, says so, and promises not to sell
     or take investment.
   - If the threshold is never reached, nothing is handed over and nothing
     is sold.
3. **One name: our.one.** It replaces "OURS at our.one" as the public name.
   OURS remains the repository's working name.
4. **The message.**
   - The front page: *"Today it's mine. At 100,000 members, I give it
     away."* The live count is shown as a rank: *"You'd be #N+1."*
   - The earlier *"it's ours"* is dropped after a cold read: a members' body
     gives control, not ownership.
   - The idea: *"Maintainers, not owners."*
   - The story: WhatsApp's arc from 2012 to 2025.
   - The honest part: an answer to *"what if it never gets there?"*, stated
     openly.
5. **The website, built under a new BUILD mandate, `M-0011`.**
   - the front page and the counter;
   - `/contract`;
   - seats that an administrator opens, and a waiting list, both off until
     a controller is named;
   - the rename to our.one;
   - a new status line;
   - the weekly email naming the most recent posters, not the most active.

## Why 100,000

- **It came from the founder.** He offered "100k or 1M".
- **A million reads as "never".**
- **Facebook's user votes (2009–2012) show the danger.** They counted only
  if 30% of all users voted, fewer than 1% did in 2012, and the votes were
  abolished.

## The alternatives weighed, in the evidence

The founder chose to prove resonance before paying for structure.

| Alternative | Why it was not chosen now |
|---|---|
| A members' association now | three people are easy to capture |
| A Czech nadační fond with a members' spolek | local, and a structure before any proof |
| The Commons Conservancy holding the domain now | free, but still a transfer before proof |

Each remains available. None is required before 100,000 under this
proposal.

## The counter-case

- **"Until 100,000 it is a promise, which is Ello again."** Partly true.
  - Ello's promise sat in a charter its buyer could delete.
  - Here, the promises are the terms each user joins under, the code is
    open, and exports work.
  - The site states what holds each promise, and says plainly that before
    100,000 it is a promise.
  - What would make it Ello is a sale, and the contract forbids one.
- **"The counter can be gamed."** Membership comes only by invitation. The
  handover counts *verified* members, as the contract will define, and the
  count is produced by open code.
- **"100,000 may never come."** Then the honest answer on the site applies.

## Unresolved, as `[CONFIRM]`

- **The controller before 100,000.** The founder personally, or a Czech
  company of his: unlimited personal liability against a company that could
  be sold with the data. Ctrl AI, a US company, adds a question about data
  transfers.
- **What counts as a "verified member"** at the handover, and who counts
  them.
- **The founding procedure** for the members' body, which must be
  published before the threshold.
- **Release prerequisites:**
  - hosting, database and email accounts;
  - `CLIENT_IP_HEADER`;
  - `our.one` DNS, after ONE moves to `oneburger.cz`;
  - a short legal review of `/privacy` and `/contract`.
