## The problem

The adopted pilot (`D-0003`, `M-0003`, `FEED-PILOT.md`) bets that a
familiar feed whose strongest difference lives in its settings will give a
professional constituency a reason to arrive. An outside reviewer, reading
the repository cold, challenges that bet on one ground the plan itself
already half-admits: **a feed asks people to form a new habit, supply
content, and find people worth following** — and explained settings, however
good, are unlikely to carry that burden. `D-0003`'s own counter-case calls
this "cold start" and accepts it as a hypothesis. The reviewer says: test
the hypothesis before building six parts on it.

The reviewer also found, in an afternoon, that the founding authority and
the first three decisions were still marked `DRAFT` while acted on — "an
unresolved credibility issue for a project whose central promise is
legitimate authority." That finding is already addressed in `P-0001` and
`D-0004`; its appearance here is evidence that the fix is urgent, not
optional.

## What the review proposes

Keep the ambition, change the entry point: OURS as **a way for an existing
community to commission and govern shared software**. Start with a
deliberately chosen professional circle the founder can personally reach;
give them a useful home for something they already do badly across
scattered tools — the reviewer's candidate is *finding collaborators and
exchanging trusted work opportunities*: a portable page (what I do, what I
need, whether I am available), a finite view of requests and opportunities
from the circle, introductions that lead to actual collaboration, control
over visibility and notifications, and a way to propose an improvement and
see its disposition. A chronological feed may support this; it must not
define the product. Before building, run a bounded **discovery and manual
service pilot** with a budget and criteria set in advance; write the
narrow product mandate from that evidence; then demonstrate correction and
continuity. Position against incumbents not on a copyable feature but on
five tests of control. Recruit people who bring a practice, not an
endorsement; give them a founding project, not a founding rank.

## Assessment

The founder asked for weighing, not defending. Here is the weighing, in
three columns.

### Where the review is right, and the plan should change

- **The entry point.** A purpose-bound surface for a circle that already
  exchanges work has what a feed lacks: a concrete outcome (an
  introduction made, a project staffed), natural scarcity (fewer posts is
  better, so no content-supply problem), a reason to return (a need, not a
  habit), and far fewer behaviors — each of which can be explained, which
  is what the thesis promises. `FEED-PILOT.md` §3 already reached for this
  ("single-player value before network value: a governed profile page…");
  the reviewer finishes the thought by naming the job.
- **The habit burden.** `FIRST-PRODUCT.md` §17 lists "the first Cell
  requires a mass network before offering personal value" as a stop
  condition. A feed is the archetype most exposed to it.
- **Test by hand before building.** Delivering the outcome manually for
  six weeks — a curated digest, facilitated introductions, one real
  tradeoff decided with the circle — is the cheapest possible test of the
  product hypothesis, and it produces something this repository has never
  had: an **observation record**, the state `OBSERVED` earned for the
  first time. The reviewer's decisive evidence is the thesis's own
  standard: repeated voluntary use, useful outcomes, willingness to
  sustain, and someone other than the founder taking operational
  responsibility — the last of which is also a sunset condition in
  `FOUNDING-AUTHORITY.md` §8.
- **The five tests of control as the public spine.** Replace the operator;
  constrain changes to the product's purpose; inspect spending and choose
  what to sustain; challenge a decision through a meaningful appeal;
  continue elsewhere with usable software and permitted data. These are
  `THESIS.md` §7's falsifiable questions, and the reviewer arrived at them
  independently. The public case leads with them; settings with receipts
  becomes the proof layer beneath, not the headline.
- **"A founding project, not a founding rank."** Already this project's
  position (the Founding Million was retired for exactly this); the phrase
  is better than ours and should be kept.
- **Funding.** Founder-funded validation, then transparent service funding
  once useful outcomes exist, every subsidy with a visible source, and a
  budget that answers *what does keeping this useful and dependable cost
  us?* — consistent with `THESIS.md` §10 and the cost column of
  `LIFECYCLE.md` §4.
- **Adjacent efforts.** Bluesky's custom feeds, Social.coop, and
  Mastodon's institutional work belong in `THESIS.md` §12 beside the prior
  art already listed, as potential collaborators. Round four's
  "govern the lens" already leaned on the AT Protocol.
- **Measure cost; do not assume cheap code.** `LIFECYCLE.md` §4 makes the
  thesis's prediction falsifiable per change. The reviewer's caution
  strengthens it.

### Where the review is partly right

- **"Incumbents can copy settings with receipts."** The *interface* is
  copyable; the *binding* is not — a receipt that points at authority
  which can refuse, reverse, and be replaced is what `D-0003` reason 3
  actually claims. But the reviewer's practical point stands: a person
  cannot tell a real receipt from a decorative one without the audit
  station. So the claim narrows: the receipt is proof for those who check;
  the five tests are the case for everyone. `D-0006` records the narrowing
  rather than editing `D-0003`.
- **"Defer code until the tests require it."** Right for Parts B–E of
  `M-0003`, which encode a product definition the pilot may change. Wrong
  for Part A (the rulebook, the option catalog, `R-OPTION-PROVENANCE`) and
  for the housekeeping in `D-0004`: those are the machine, not the product,
  they have no dependency on which circle is chosen, and delaying them
  costs credibility with no evidence gained. **Part A and the manual pilot
  run in parallel.**

### What the review misses

- **The manual pilot handles personal data.** Availability, needs, contact
  details, and who was introduced to whom are personal data the moment the
  founder writes them down — in a spreadsheet as much as in a database.
  The review's pilot has no controller, no consent, no retention, no
  deletion path. `D-0003` was explicit that nothing may invite anyone or
  name a controller under `M-0003`; the same honesty applies to a manual
  service. The pilot therefore needs its own authority — an `OPERATE`
  mandate held by the founder as steward, naming what is collected, where
  it lives, for how long, who holds it (`[CONFIRM]` — the founder
  personally or the transitional operator), and that every participant
  consented and can leave with their data.
- **The workflow sketch is itself a guess.** The reviewer says so; it is
  repeated here so nobody mistakes the proposal for validation. "Work
  opportunities within a circle" has incumbents too — the differentiator
  remains control, and the five tests apply to it exactly as to a feed.
- **A concierge service can become a job, not a product.** Six weeks of
  the founder's hours are the pilot's real cost and must be budgeted and
  recorded (`LIFECYCLE.md` §4) before the first invitation, with a stop
  rule if the hours exceed the budget.
- **The METR citation is unconfirmed** and is not used in any public text
  until a named human confirms what it says (`R-CHECKABLE-CLAIM`).

## Requested decision — `D-0006`, CHARTER

1. **Amend the feed Cell's mission** (the charter table in `D-0003`):
   *the shared surface of a professional circle — who does what, who needs
   what, who is available; a finite view of requests and opportunities;
   introductions — governed by the people it serves. A chronological feed
   engine supports it; it does not define it.* The Cell keeps the
   identifier `feed`; the product's name stays undecided (`D-0003`
   prohibits an unadopted product-line name).
2. **Re-sequence `M-0003`:** Part A proceeds now. Parts B–E are held. Part
   F keeps its role. After the pilot's observation record, Parts B–E are
   superseded by a narrower mandate written from that evidence.
3. **Authorize `M-0005`, an `OPERATE` mandate** held by the founder as
   steward — the discovery and manual service pilot, run through the ring:
   - a circle of 15–30 reachable professionals who already exchange work
     informally; the founder names it before the first invitation;
   - six weeks; founder time budgeted at six hours a week, recorded as
     cost; stop if exceeded by half;
   - data: name, what I do, what I need, availability, contact — one
     founder-controlled store; consent at invitation; deletion on request;
     retention to the pilot's end plus thirty days; controller `[CONFIRM]`;
   - delivered by hand: a weekly digest of requests and availability;
     introductions facilitated; requests curated;
   - **one real tradeoff decided with the circle** — visibility of
     opportunities versus privacy of availability — as a proposal and a
     decision with dissent preserved, and its digest cadence recorded as a
     `GOVERNED` default with the founder-bootstrap label;
   - **criteria set before the first invitation** (numbers are the
     founder's to adjust, and are hypotheses): at least half of
     participants contribute in weeks one to two and again in weeks five
     to six; at least five introductions accepted, at least two leading
     to actual collaboration by week eight; at least three participants
     state, with a number, what they would pay or contribute to sustain
     it; at least one participant takes an operational role for two
     weeks; one change reversed on request, receipted;
   - output: `O-0001`, the first observation record, closing the
     hypotheses of `M-0003` and `M-0005` as `CONFIRMED`, `REFUTED`, or
     `UNTESTED` — and, from it, the mandate that replaces Parts B–E.
4. **The public case's spine is the five tests of control.** Candidate
   lines recorded as working material, `THESIS.md` §15 register, not
   adopted copy: *The software we live in should be OURS.* — *Bring
   something your community already does together. Let's make the software
   answer to you.* — *Here is where we started. Come build what comes
   next.*
5. **Prior art:** add Bluesky custom feeds, Social.coop, and Mastodon's
   institutional work to `THESIS.md` §12, with links confirmed by the
   founder before the amendment text is adopted.
6. **Narrow `D-0003` reason 3** as recorded above: the receipt is proof
   for those who check; the five tests are the case for everyone.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Counter-case |
|---|---|
| keep the pilot as adopted — build all six parts now | the habit burden is real and unmeasured; six parts encode a product definition nobody has tested; the counter-case in `D-0003` becomes the outcome |
| build the circle's workflow product directly, skipping the manual pilot | still a guess, now with code behind it; the manual pilot costs hours, not months, and yields the first `OBSERVED` |
| abandon the feed engine altogether | the review's own workflow needs a profile, typed posts (requests), a finite view, settings, and propose-an-improvement — which is Parts A–E with a narrower Part B; Part A is product-agnostic |
| pause everything for discovery | Part A and the `D-0004` housekeeping have no dependency on the circle, and their delay costs credibility with no evidence gained |
| run the manual pilot informally, without a mandate or a controller | the pilot handles personal data from day one; running it outside the ring would be the first OURS activity to touch people while bypassing the machine |

## Cost and risk

Proposal: three hours, `ESTIMATED`. The manual pilot: about thirty-six
founder hours over six weeks, `ESTIMATED`, recorded weekly. Part A: agent
cost recorded in its receipt. Risk: a concierge pilot that never becomes
software (bounded by the budget and the stop rule); personal data held by
hand (bounded by the `OPERATE` mandate's terms); the feed engine built for
a product that changes shape (bounded by holding Parts B–E).
