**Status:** ADOPTED, by the founder's words of 2 October 2026, as recorded
in [`D-0020`](../decisions/D-0020.md). D-0020 says which parts it reads as
an interpretation, and what is still open.
**Requests:** D-0020 (CONSTITUTIONAL)

**Drafted by:** an agent, from the founder's words, the review they chose,

## The problem

By 2 October the records described more than the site showed.

- **The front page is still the feed's.** Its headline, *"Just your people.
  Then you're done."*, is the feed's promise. The network the feed belongs
  to, the common agreement and the builders' invitation are behind footer
  links.
- **The builders' pages lead with rules.** `/build` and `/maintainers`
  explain participation mostly through what's checked and what's
  forbidden.
- **The kit repeats "build first, find people later".** `build.md` orders
  the work as questions, setup, build, check, then the proposal. A builder
  invests in code before learning whether anyone wants it, which is the
  problem the founder named in D-0019's words.
- **The message test tests the old definition.** Its "understood" counts
  *"a social network or feed for your friends"*, and its "different"
  counts *"nobody owns it"*, a misreading of the definition D-0017 §B
  gives.

The founder asked another agent for a fresh view. It wrote a review and a
design study. The founder's verdict: *"his view, design and message is much
much better ... help me then fully rework user experience to this one"*.

## What is proposed

1. **The message:** *"The software we live in should be ours."* Under it:
   *"We're bringing people and builders together to create services their
   users can control. Starting with a friends feed. Building toward much
   more."* Above it: *"AI helps us build. Together, we can make it ours."*
2. **A front door with two entrances:** one for people who want something
   better, one for people who can build it.
3. **The feed as the first project,** with other possibilities labelled as
   possibilities, and the feed's own page, with its words and its join
   form, one step away.
4. **An illustration of the idea:** the people who maintain a service
   change, and the people who use it, their relationships and their rules
   stay. It says our.one can't do this today.
5. **Builders: "Build something people can depend on."** The deal, with
   its pay conditional on people choosing and funding a service; the line
   for a coding agent; and **"Draft an idea first"**: an idea, and how to
   find out whether people want it, before any code.
6. **Needs, too:** anyone can draft what they depend on, what they'd change,
   and what would make an alternative worth trying.
7. **In the open:** what exists, what is a draft, and what isn't built.
8. **An identity of its own** for the public pages: warm paper, dark green
   ink, an acid green field, a rust accent, large type. The signed-in feed
   keeps its own look.
9. **A revised message test,** for the wider proposition, with both
   potential users and builders.

## What it does not do

- It doesn't change the rules, the check or what passing means (D-0019).
- It takes needs and ideas by email only, read by hand (D-0018 §D). A
  draft written on the site stays in the visitor's browser until they copy
  it or send it themselves.
- It doesn't change the feed's verified words, its contract or the common
  agreement's draft.
- It doesn't deploy. M-0012 stays a draft until the founder's go.

## Risks

- **A message chosen without strangers.** The founder chose it on
  judgment. The revised test still runs before the public launch, and its
  results can change the words (D-0020 §C).
- **A bigger promise.** "Should be ours" can be heard as "is ours". Every
  place that names user control says it isn't built yet, and the claims
  scan reads the new pages.
- **Possibilities read as products.** Each is labelled, with no project
  announced.
- **A new line for coding agents** that nobody has tried. It is tried by a
  fresh agent before release, as the first one was.
