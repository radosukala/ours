## The problem

`P-0003` moved the entry point from *a feed with explained settings* to *a
professional circle's shared surface*. The founder's reflection of 7
September goes one step further and, in doing so, arrives back at the
thesis: not one OURS product built app by app, but **a way for
communities to build, use, govern, and own the software they need** —
small products for real groups, many of them, none of them a tenth
thousand Instagram. The founder names what is still missing: a
simplification that ordinary people can join — a clear process and a
simple face over it.

The reviewer's second reply gives that simplification a shape: the shared
need as the unit; a home organised around five ordinary questions;
ownership made concrete through actions rather than certificates; the
promise *cannot be bypassed* replaced by specific protections each with an
honest account of what holds it; a protocol underneath, a service in
front; shared software families with local authority; paid builders and no
marketplace yet; and an assisted "start something together" experience
built around one real shared tool.

## Assessment

### Where the founder and the reviewer are right

- **This is the thesis returning to itself.** `THESIS.md` §8 — one root
  institution, many Product Cells governed by the people they serve — is
  exactly *communities building and owning the software they need*. The
  feed was a demonstration vehicle. This removes the vehicle.
- **The unit — a shared need with identifiable people, recurring use,
  concrete consequences, and rules worth deciding — is the best-scoped
  first product in this record.** It does not fight incumbents; its
  network effects are local; its cold start is one group; its behaviors
  are few enough that each can be explained, which is the promise.
- **"Coordination that produces little new code counts as success."** This
  is the sentence that keeps OURS from becoming a gallery of generated
  apps, and the cost column of `LIFECYCLE.md` §4 is how it is checked.
- **The five questions** — Use, Improve, People & rules, Money, Continuity
  — are the ledger with human labels. `SHARED-TOOL.md` §4 maps each to the
  ring stations beneath it.
- **Ownership through actions, with unresolved answers shown** is
  `THESIS.md` §7 and `R-NO-FICTIONAL-OWNERSHIP` rendered as product.
- **Shared software families with local authority** is the federal
  architecture's economics, stated as a testable hypothesis with its
  boundary named.
- **Paid builders, no marketplace yet; a fiscal host before payments;
  founder-funded validation first** — consistent with `THESIS.md` §10 and
  the threat model's *adoption failure* and *steward capture*.

### Where the record must sharpen what was said

- **"Community ownership that cannot be bypassed" is a claim this project
  already forbids itself** (`R-WEAKEST-LAYER`; `AGENTS.md` §9). The
  reviewer is right to replace it, and the replacement is our own law:
  meaningful control, specific protections, each with its holding class
  stated. The founder's ambition survives intact; the absolute does not.
- **"Millions of community members" and "plenty of builders"** are hopes,
  and are recorded as hypotheses, not premises (`R-CHECKABLE-CLAIM`).
- **"A system in which communities can build software" is generalised
  machinery**, and `AGENTS.md` §12 forbids building it before one real
  loop needs it. The reviewer's own sequencing agrees: assist one founding
  by hand, then extract what repeats. The product sentence is the north
  star; the first build is one group's tool.
- **The first community must be one where the founder is a member, not
  the boss.** A group the founder controls cannot test whether control
  transfers. `SHARED-TOOL.md` §10 adds this to the selection criteria.
- **"Protocol" must not attract the pattern it disclaims.** The protocol
  here is record formats and authority semantics with a verifier — like
  git or a feed format — not a token or a chain (`THESIS.md` §13). Its
  test is portability, and it is extracted, not designed.
- **Supersede the feed cleanly rather than hold it.** Nothing was built
  under `D-0003`, `M-0003`, or `D-0005`. Superseding them now costs
  nothing but the record and exercises the correction station for the
  first time. Their Cell-agnostic engine (`FEED-PILOT.md` §4–§6) is
  carried forward as `SHARED-TOOL.md` §11.
- **Two AI reviewers agreeing is not evidence.** This proposal is drafted
  by one and quotes another. The founder asked for outside perspective
  and got it; the only evidence that counts is the first community saying
  the sentence in `SHARED-TOOL.md` §10.

## Requested decision — `D-0006`, CONSTITUTIONAL

1. **Adopt `foundation/SHARED-TOOL.md` as direction.** The product
   sentence and the movement line are working material, not adopted copy.
2. **Supersede the feed pilot:** `D-0003`, `M-0003`, and `D-0005` become
   `SUPERSEDED`, retained in full; `FEED-PILOT.md` is marked `SUPERSEDED`
   with §4–§6 carried forward. Reason recorded: the feed becomes one
   possible shared tool a community may ask for, and none has.
3. **Authorize `M-0005`, an `OPERATE` mandate** held by the founder as
   steward: assist one real community's founding by hand, through the
   ring, per `SHARED-TOOL.md` §10 steps 1–4 and 6–7 — with the terms
   `P-0003` set out and this proposal keeps: a budget of founder hours
   recorded weekly with a stop rule; what personal data is collected,
   where it lives, for how long, who holds it (`[CONFIRM]`); consent at
   invitation; deletion on request; one real tradeoff decided with the
   group, dissent preserved; success criteria set before the first
   invitation; output `O-0001`, the first observation record.
4. **Authorize `M-0006`, a `BUILD` mandate** for the Cell-agnostic
   machinery that step 5 needs and that no community's choice changes:
   the `ours.option/v0.1` record and `R-OPTION-PROVENANCE` (from `M-0003`
   Part A, generalised); an `ours.charter/v0.1` record with a readable
   template answering purpose, participation, decisions, money, privacy,
   and continuity; CLI reporting for both. The first tool's own build
   mandate is written after step 4, from the community's decision.
5. **Amend `FIRST-PRODUCT.md` §15** with the selection criteria in
   `SHARED-TOOL.md` §10, and **`THESIS.md` §12** with Bluesky custom
   feeds, Social.coop, Mastodon's institutional work, and Open
   Collective — links confirmed by the founder before the amendment text
   is adopted.
6. **`D-0004` stands.** Each community's tool lives at its own subdomain
   of `our.one`; the institution stays at `oursorg.com`.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Counter-case |
|---|---|
| keep the feed pilot as adopted | the habit burden and the market size the founder now names; a demonstration vehicle where none is needed |
| `P-0003` as filed — a professional circle's work-opportunities surface | a better entry point than the feed, but still a product OURS chooses for a group rather than a need the group brings; superseded by the general form |
| build the "system for communities" now | generalised machinery before one loop; the reviewer's own sequencing says assist one founding first |
| publish the protocol first | a specification nobody has used; the protocol is extracted from real foundings, and a published spec would invite the token pattern-match |
| hold the feed Cell rather than supersede it | a chartered Cell with no members and no product is clutter that a reader must explain away; supersession is honest and free |
| skip the machinery mandate and do everything by hand | step 5 needs the option and charter records to exist so the first tool's settings carry provenance; the machinery is small and Cell-agnostic |

## Cost and risk

Proposal: four hours, `ESTIMATED`. `M-0005`: founder hours, budgeted in
the mandate. `M-0006`: a few days of agent work, recorded. Risk: the
founder assists one founding well and cannot make it repeatable — bounded
by `SHARED-TOOL.md` §14, which says that outcome is worth knowing; a
community chosen for reachability rather than need — bounded by the
selection criteria; the "protocol" word attracting the wrong audience —
bounded by not publishing one.
