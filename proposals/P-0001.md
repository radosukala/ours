## The problem

Two problems, one decision, because both change what a person sees when
they ask *where is OURS, and how does it decide?*

**1. The address.** The founder locked `OURS · oursorg.com` on 30 August
2026, when `our.one` served the ONE food business and was listed in the
founding authority as *not a project asset*. Two facts have since changed:
the founder is willing to transfer `our.one` to the institution, and a
product is about to exist. `org` is accurate for an institution and
awkward for a product; two addresses risk confusion; and a brand that is
also a dotted domain cannot be spoken.

**2. The head of the chain.** Decisions `D-0000` to `D-0003` have no
proposal and no preserved deliberation; adoption was never recorded as an
event; cost is not recorded anywhere; and the complete lifecycle of a
change is described in no document a user can read. The thesis's claim
that code is the cheapest part of a change is, today, only a claim.

## Affected

The founder (a personal asset becomes a project asset); the ONE business
(a domain cutover); the future member institution (brand and asset); the
feed pilot cohort (the product's address); agents (record fields and
envelope rules).

## Alternatives considered

### Address

| | Option | Counter-case |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | two-level: `oursorg.com` for the institution, `our.one` for products | architecturally clear, but a person is handed three things to hold — `OURS`, `our.one`, `oursorg.com` — and the founder's confusion worry is real |
| **A2** | **`our.one` for everything; `oursorg.com` redirects** — *recommended* | concentrates identity on one domain under one registry (`.one` is operated by One.com A/S, a hosting company; the TLD dates from 2014); `.com` typing leakage goes to `ourone.com`, which is broker-parked, not an operating business |
| A3 | status quo: `oursorg.com` only | no cutover risk; `org` stays awkward on every product; `our.one` idle |

### Brand

| | Option | Counter-case |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | **OURS** — *recommended* | a common word, unwinnable in search, defended by writing discipline rather than trademark — all known since 30 August, none changed |
| B2 | OUR ONE | reads as *the chosen one*; collides with the founder's own ONE food brand and with many *One* marks (One.com, OnePlus, Capital One); singular where the thesis is plural; weaker as a claim |
| B3 | `our.one` as the brand, dotted | nothing mismatches, because the name is the address — but it cannot be said without *dot*, reads as a URL inside a sentence, couples the institution's name to one registry's contract, and forfeits the word that carries the thesis |

The mismatch under B1 + A2 is one letter, and it reads as a sentence:
*OURS — at our.one.* A test the founder can run before deciding: say
*OURS, at our dot one* and *OURS, at oursorg dot com* to someone who has
never heard of the project, and ask them to type it.

### Address — alternatives added on 4 September 2026, from the aftermarket

The founder checked what an exact-word address would cost before locking
`our.one`. Prices are the listings the founder saw; availability decays.

| | Option | Counter-case |
|---|---|---|
| **A4** | **`ours.world`, listed at $500** | the exact word, on a generic gTLD (registry Binky Moon, LLC — Identity Digital), with no dependency on the ONE cutover — it removes both costs of `our.one` at once; against it: it reads grander than the house voice, `oursworld.com` is broker-parked so typing leakage lands on a sales page, and a new-gTLD sender reputation argues for sending sign-in emails from a `.com` regardless |
| A5 | `ours.live`, listed at $500 | the exact word, but `.live` means streaming and misleads on sight |
| A6 | `ours.ws`, free | `.ws` is Samoa's country code, administered by the Government of Samoa — an institution's name under another state's policy; a reputation among low-cost registrations; *Ours World System* is a backronym, and a name that needs a sentence loses; it resolves to a parking address today, so availability is to be confirmed |
| A7 | `theours.co`, free | *the ours* is not a phrase anyone says; `.co` is Colombia's country code with `.com` leakage |
| A8 | `oursare.com` $500, `oursclub.com` $600 | fragments — *ours are* what? — and a word that says exclusivity to a project whose membership is meant to be open |

**Two finalists, then.** `our.one`: shortest, already owned, one letter
away from the brand, coupled to the ONE cutover. `ours.world`: the exact
word, $500, independent of any other business's migration, a shade grand.
The agent's lean is to `ours.world`, because it removes the founder's
standing worry and decouples the launch from a domain move that has its
own checklist and its own timetable. The founder's ear decides. Whichever
is chosen: **the ONE cutover must not be a dependency of the OURS launch.**

### Address — alternatives added on 5 September 2026, from the founder's second search

| | Option | Counter-case |
|---|---|---|
| A9 | verb-prefixed names — `claimours.com` and `oursplatform.com` (free `.com`); `theours.co`, `beours.co`, `isours.co`, `meetours.co`, `goours.co` (free `.co`) | each moves the name from a word to a phrase — *claim ours*, *be ours*, *go ours* — which is a campaign line, not an address, and the brand has to be un-prefixed to be found; *claim* reads as an airdrop; *platform* is a description; and the `.com` sibling of every `.co` candidate already belongs to someone else (`theours.com`, `beours.com`, `isours.com`, `meetours.com`, `goours.com` are all registered), so typing leakage lands on other people's sites rather than a broker's page |

**On `.co`.** Better than `.ws`: a globally marketed, well-run registry
with an ordinary reputation. But it is still Colombia's country code,
administered under the Ministry of Information and Communications
Technologies, so it carries the same class of risk as `.ws` at a lower
reputational cost — and it says *company*, which is the one thing OURS is
trying not to be as its identity.

**The tiering that keeps the recommendation stable:** the exact word on a
generic TLD (`ours.world`); then the owned near-word (`our.one`); then any
modified word on any TLD. The second search's candidates all fall in the
third tier. Finalists unchanged.

### Brand and address — alternatives added on 5 September 2026

| | Option | Counter-case |
|---|---|---|
| B4 | rename the brand to **OUR**, to match `our.one` | *our* is a determiner, not a name: it needs a noun and cannot stand alone, so every sentence that uses it as a name asks *our what?*; it dissolves into the project's own copy (*our members*, *our rules*), where no capitalisation rescues it; it cannot end a sentence, so it cannot carry the thesis's claim; in speech it collides with *are* and *hour*; and given the address, people will say *our one* — which is B2, already declined. OURS is a pronoun: it stands alone, ends the sentence, and asserts ownership against someone else's. **OUR describes; OURS asserts.** |
| A10 | `ours.world` as the canonical address, with `our.one` kept as the short-link domain (`our.one/feed` → `feed.ours.world`) | gives `our.one` a daily role without making it the identity, removes the one-letter gap from every surface, and removes the ONE cutover from the launch path; the cost is $500 and a second domain on the transfer list |

Two notes for the record. First, brand ≠ domain-name-part has precedent
where the domain is short and seen constantly — Bitly at `bit.ly`,
Instagram at `instagr.am` in its first years — so `OURS · our.one` is
survivable; it is the founder's ear, not the rule, that is uneasy. Second,
a name should not be chosen by a registry's inventory: a domain is an
asset that can be lost, repriced, or transferred; the name is the
institution. Fit the address to the name, never the name to the address.
