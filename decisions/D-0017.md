## The decision

### A. A framework, with the feed as its first project

**our.one becomes a network of services:**

- their users fund them;
- their users will control them;
- maintainers build, improve and run them.

**The feed is the first project, under the same framework.** Its maintainer
is the founder, unpaid by declared choice: "not paid for now".

**Two invitations,** open from the public launch:

- builders propose a service;
- people name a need, with or without using the feed.

**The framework does not depend on size.** A volunteer's tool and a service
with a paid team are covered by the same agreement.

### B. User ownership, defined by decisions

> **Owned by its users means: its users, together, decide its essential
> rules, approve its budget, and can change who runs it while the service
> keeps going. Nobody can sell that away from them, and each person can
> always take their own data and leave.**

**The common agreement must establish, under that sentence:**

- **Collective authority:** a defined membership and a practical process for
  the purpose, the important rules, the budget and the appointment of the
  maintainer. Ordinary product work is delegated.
- **Continuity:** the holder keeps what a successor needs: the code licence,
  the name and domain, the permitted data, recovery and administration, and
  the funds. Apache-2.0 §6 grants no trademark rights, so the licence alone
  does not secure the name.
- **Individual rights:** no majority can expose or sell a person's data or
  private connections.
- **No sale of control.**

**The status, stated wherever the definition appears:**

- None of the collective rights is in force on our.one yet.
- The definition gives the word a meaning. It claims ownership of nothing
  today.
- D-0012's prohibition on present-tense ownership claims stands.

### C. The agreement works both ways

| Users get | Maintainers get |
|---|---|
| A defined service, its important rules under their control | A defined scope and freedom over ordinary product decisions |
| An agreed operating budget and visible pay | Agreed pay once funded, a term and renewal |
| The right to change who runs it | Fair notice, fair termination terms, a dispute process |
| A service that survives its maintainer leaving | Attribution, and a reputation they keep |

**Builders keep:** the copyright in their code. They grant the rights a
successor needs.

**Builders give up:**

- selling the service, or its users;
- investors expecting a return from it;
- the users' list. They get scoped, revocable access instead.

### D. Two kinds of project

- **An independent experiment.** Its maintainer holds the accounts, the
  data, the name and the deploy. It can be listed only as independent, and
  it makes none of our.one's ownership claims.
- **A protected service.** A holder keeps the name, the domain, the data,
  the deploy and the funds, and the maintainer works with revocable access.
  - **Its test:** can an appointed successor continue it, with the permitted
    records and relationships, without the former maintainer able to block
    it?

**The feed is a protected service with one stated exception:** until the
holder exists, the founder holds what the holder will hold.

### E. Traction is the first funded service

- **The holder is set up when the first service is funded by its users.**
  - It must be able to act as data controller and to hold funds.
  - Its legal form is open (§K).
- **Each service's schedule names its route to user control:**
  - its constituency;
  - its decision process;
  - when the collective rights start.
- **100,000 stays the feed's trigger,** as D-0012 §B and D-0013 §B set it.

### F. Money

- **Shared costs are public, and approved by the people who pay them.** They
  follow actual responsibilities. This replaces the draft promise "our.one
  never takes a cut".
- **Nobody invests in any service for a return.** This is promise 1's rule,
  applied to every service.
- **Revenue is not income.** Any illustration states gross revenue, and what
  it must cover.

### G. From interest to customers

**A proposal states:**

- the need, and the experience it offers;
- what users would have to change or move;
- the price, the scope and the operating budget, including the
  maintainer's pay;
- what it asks for now;
- what has to happen before work starts, and what happens if it doesn't.

**Interest, a trial and an intention to buy are counted separately.** None
of them is a payment.

**Naming a need:**

- it takes a confirmed address, not an account;
- it does not count toward 100,000.

**No money moves through our.one** until a holder exists.

### H. The public launch

**The launch carries the framework in readable words:**

- the proposition;
- the common agreement, marked *"being developed: none of the collective
  rights are in force yet"*;
- the feed's project page;
- the two invitations;
- a route from a proposal to a trial to a funded operating agreement, run
  by hand.

**Not built until a real proposal needs it:** marketplace software.

**Deploying the feed under M-0012 stays separate from the launch.**

- M-0012 deploys with its seats closed.
- The public launch is the opening of seats and the announcement. It waits
  for the framework pages, which need a later decision and a BUILD mandate
  of their own.

**The message is tested before the front page changes.** Five strangers see
two lines against today's headline:

- *"Apps that won't be sold out from under you. The first is a feed of just
  your people. Pitch the next one."*
- *"We're building our.one so people can bring the software they want into
  existence, and keep control of it."*

The first line holds only for protected services.

### I. The data line

**Adopted, as the founder's requirement:**

- a maintainer must not be able to take members' data for their own use or
  for sale;
- members' data and connections are kept centrally by the holder, so that
  no single app is a point of failure.

**Proposed, not yet adopted (§K): seven safeguards for every protected
service.** They follow THE-LAYER §6's data contract and §7.1's runtime
boundary:

| Safeguard | Today | Class when built |
|---|---|---|
| The law: the holder as controller, each maintainer a processor (GDPR Art. 28) | `DECLARED`; no holder or processing agreement exists | `INTERPRETED` |
| The agreement: no sale, no use beyond the service | `DECLARED` | `INTERPRETED` |
| No keys: no database credentials or production secrets for maintainers | Not built; the founder holds every key | `ENFORCED` by custody |
| Reach: an app reads and writes only what its contract names | Static check `S-CONTRACT-DECLARED` exists, tested on a fixture only; runtime capability not built | `ENFORCED` |
| Leave: data leaves only to named origins, in named fields | Not built; M-0007 is DRAFT | `ENFORCED` at the boundary · `INTERPRETED` beyond |
| The record: every read and disclosure logged, visible to the person | Not built | `CHECKED` |
| Custody: the holder owns the accounts, the database and the backups | No holder exists | legal control `DECLARED` |

**What no safeguard prevents:** misuse of what an app may show or send.
That can be recorded and challenged, not prevented. Public wording says
*"can't"* only where code makes it true.

## Prohibited under this decision

- **Present-tense ownership claims,** which D-0012 already prohibits.
  "Owned" is used only as §B defines it, with its status.
- **Stating any safeguard of §I as built before it is.**
- **Giving any service or maintainer** members' data or connections before
  §I's safeguards exist for it.
- **Taking money through our.one** before a holder exists.
- **Promising builders income.** "Can earn a living" stays conditional on
  people choosing and paying for the service.

## Reasons

1. **Builders are short of attention and income,** not of the ability to
   build.
   - RevenueCat's 2026 report puts the median subscription app at about $72
     a month a year after launch, across apps already earning.
   - Tidelift's 2024 survey found 60% of the 437 maintainers it surveyed
     unpaid.

   A framework that brings funded demand to builders answers the founder's
   point.
2. **Paid users and paid developers are not enough on their own.**
   - App.net had both, and closed in 2017 over "the chicken-and-egg issue".
   - The June our.one had a membership and a public ledger, and "nobody
     came".

   So the first proof is concrete: a builder chooses the terms, people
   choose and pay, the funding renews, and a change of maintainer is
   survived.
3. **Ownership without authority over rules and money is a user
   interface.** THE-LAYER §7 says so of keys and assets. The outside review
   says so of rules and budgets.
4. **Interpretations are written down,** so they can be corrected.

The sources for these reasons are in P-0011's evidence. The architect's
working drafts hold the rest, and they are not published.
