## The decision

### A. The message, and the front door

**The front page becomes the network's front door.** It carries:

- **The message:** *"The software we live in should be ours."* Under it:
  *"We're bringing people and builders together to create services their
  users can control. Starting with a friends feed. Building toward much
  more."* Above it: *"AI helps us build. Together, we can make it ours."*
- **Two entrances on the first screen:** one for people who want something
  better, one for people who can build it.
- **The feed as the first project.** Other kinds of service are shown only
  as possibilities, each labelled, with no project announced.
- **An illustration of the idea:** the maintainer changes, and the people,
  their relationships and their rules stay. It says that it is an
  illustration, and that our.one can't do this today.
- **What "ours" means:** a say in the rules, the money and who runs a
  service, as the common agreement proposes, shown as proposed.
- **The builders' invitation:** *"Build something people can depend on."*
  The deal, with pay only if people choose and fund the service; an idea
  before any code (§D); and the line for a coding agent.
- **In the open:** what is built, what is a draft, and what isn't built,
  with links to the code, the costs, the agreement and who holds power.
- **Find your part:** start with the feed, name a need, or bring an idea.

**The public pages take an identity of their own:** warm paper, dark green
ink, an acid green field, a rust accent and large type, in the fonts the
visitor's device already has. Nothing loads from anywhere else. A dark
version follows the device's setting.

### B. Where everything goes

- **`/`:** the front door (§A).
- **`/feed`:** the feed's own page. It keeps D-0015's and D-0016's words,
  word for word: the headline *"Just your people. Then you're done."*, the
  lede, the join form and its seats, the picture of the feed, the signed
  promise, the 7% finding, how it works, who runs it and the questions.
  - *Interpretation:* the founder chose a new front door, not new words for
    the feed. Its verified words move with it unchanged.
- **The header:** the idea, projects, building and what's in the open, and
  "Sign in", on every public page.
- **`/projects`:** the feed, with its facts, and the labelled
  possibilities.
- **`/build`:** led by the builders' invitation and the idea (§D), then the
  line for a coding agent, the rules, what the check can and can't tell,
  and the tools.
- **`/maintainers`:** the job and its terms, as now, and the two drafts
  (§E).
- **Every other public page** takes the identity, with its words
  unchanged, except `/privacy` (§E).
- **The signed-in app keeps its own look.** The feed is a project, and its
  look is its own.

### C. The message test

- **The founder chose the message on judgment, without the test.**
  *Interpretation:* this record reads *"fully rework user experience to
  this one"* as lifting D-0018 §C's hold on the front page's headline and
  lede, and the prohibition that went with it.
- **It doesn't read those words as dropping the test.** D-0017 §L records
  the founder's "yes" to running it. So the test is revised, as the review
  proposes, and **run on the new pages before the public launch**: the
  opening of seats and the announcement (D-0017 §H). Its results are
  recorded, and they can change the words by a later decision.
- **The revised test:**
  - *Understood:* the person describes people and builders making
    services that the people using them can control, with the feed as the
    first.
  - *Asked too:* what the users would control; what a builder would get;
    and what they think is available today.
  - *Who:* people who might use it, and people who might build.
  - *Not counted as understanding:* *"nobody owns it"*. It misreads
    D-0017 §B.
  - Five strangers can expose confusion. They can't show that it will
    spread (AGENTS.md §9).

### D. An idea comes before the code

- **A builder can start with an idea,** before any code:
  - what they'd build, and for whom;
  - what those people use today;
  - how they'd find out whether those people want it.
- **Anyone can name a need:**
  - what they depend on;
  - what they'd change;
  - what would make an alternative worth trying.
- **Both still come by email, and a person reads each one** (D-0018 §D).
  - Interest isn't an audience, and it isn't funding.
  - Interest, a trial and an intention to buy are counted separately
    (D-0017 §G).
- **The kit's instructions start with the idea.** `build.md`:
  1. asks the person;
  2. drafts the idea in `PITCH.md`, before any code;
  3. asks the person whether to find out first or to build now;
  4. only then sets the project up, builds and checks it, and finishes the
     proposal.
- **The line for a coding agent becomes:** *"Read
  https://our.one/build.md and follow it to help me bring my idea to
  our.one."* A fresh agent tries it before release, as M-0016's trial
  tried the first line.
- **The rules, the check and what passing means don't change** (D-0019).
- **The "On your own" column is retired.**
  - *Interpretation:* D-0019 set the two ways side by side because the
    founder asked for the choice to be obvious. The review the founder now
    chose says our.one should explain its own deal rather than describe
    the other way in absolutes.
  - So `/build` makes the choice by stating our.one's deal, and says
    nothing absolute about building alone.

### E. The drafts

- **On the front door, `/build` and `/maintainers`,** a visitor can draft a
  need or an idea, in their browser:
  - nothing is sent, stored or counted;
  - they can copy the draft;
  - when `PROPOSALS_EMAIL` is set, they can open it in their own email app,
    and send it themselves.
- **No form posts a draft, and no database holds one.** Email stays the
  only route (D-0018, D-0019).
- **Without JavaScript,** the same buttons lead to `/maintainers`, which says
  how to write.
- **`/privacy` says what the drafts do:** nothing leaves the browser until
  the visitor sends it.

### F. Words held to the records

- **Nothing says user control exists.** The front door says: *"Founder-led
  today. User control isn't built yet."*
- **The agreement's rights are shown as proposed,** in a draft nobody has
  signed, wherever they appear.
- **A status stays true on the deployed site and off it.** No new line says
  "not deployed", or anything else that turns false at the deploy.
- **Pay is never promised.** It depends on people choosing and funding a
  service (D-0017).
- **The claims scan reads every new page and component.**

## Prohibited under this decision

- **Saying that user control, the holder or any safeguard exists** before
  it does.
- **Presenting a possibility as a project,** or the illustration as
  something our.one can do.
- **Taking needs or ideas by any route but email,** or storing a draft.
- **Changing the feed's verified words** under M-0017: its headline, its
  lede, its promises.
- **Promising builders an audience or an income.**

## Reasons

1. **The founder chose it.** The front page told a smaller story than the
   records, and the founder judged the review's message, design and view
   better than what was built.
2. **The participation model was the larger flaw.** A builder who builds
   before anyone wants the work repeats what five launches without users
   showed (D-0015). An idea that comes first, with how to find out, costs a
   builder an afternoon, not months.
3. **The test is kept, and made useful.** A test that rewards *"a feed for
   your friends"* would have kept the narrow message. Run before the
   launch, the revised test can still change the words.
4. **The words stay true.** The bigger message comes with its status
   everywhere: founder-led today, user control not built.
