# D-0000 — Construct OURS Kernel 0.1

**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting founder adoption
**Class:** CONSTITUTIONAL
**Authority:** founder bootstrap
**Date drafted:** 30 August 2026
**Machine projection:** [`D-0000.yaml`](./D-0000.yaml)

## The decision

Construct OURS Kernel 0.1: a deterministic authority-and-mandate validator,
an offline verifier, and the record schemas they operate on.

## Why

OURS claims that legitimate human authority can be carried, without a break,
to the software that actually runs. Nothing in this repository yet tests that
claim. Until a machine can reject an invalid authority chain, the claim is
prose.

Kernel 0.1 is the smallest artifact that can make it falsifiable.

## What this decision authorizes

One mandate of class `BUILD`: `M-0000`.

## What it does not authorize

- deployment of anything, to any environment;
- registering, moving, or repointing any domain;
- any change to the legacy `ourstoday` repository or its live deployment;
- forming a legal entity, or any statement about one;
- issuing membership, or any claim that members exist;
- a database, authentication, voting interface, treasury, or blockchain;
- a generalized governance language.

## The counter-case

Recorded because a decision with no recorded counter-case is an announcement.

**The kernel is not the risk.** The predecessor project failed for want of
demand, not for want of engineering discipline. Building a validator is work
the founder is demonstrably able to do; whether anyone wants a governed
product is unknown and untested. This decision therefore reduces the smaller
risk first, and does so knowingly.

**Mitigation adopted:** the comprehension review in `FIRST-PRODUCT.md` §14 is
brought forward. It runs on paper, against the thesis, before the kernel is
finished — not after. Five people who do not know the project read the claim
and say whether they want it.

**Second objection.** Two shipping products — Senordo and DAP — already
compile institutional policy into verified, hash-chained systems. The novelty
here is narrower than "the compiler": those compile *management's* authority
for management's benefit. OURS compiles the authority of the people the
software governs. That distinction is the whole claim, and the thesis must be
sharpened to say only that.

## Reasons

1. The chain must be machine-checkable before it is publicly claimed.
2. A refusal is the product. The kernel exists to say no, and to show why.
3. Bootstrapping a compiler from a manual seed is a known, honest technique,
   and the seed's privileged status stays visible rather than being hidden.
4. Deterministic validation needs no database, no network, and no account,
   which keeps the first proof independently checkable by a stranger.

reader mistakes a single-participant decision for a consensus.
