## The decision

### A. A maintainer, not an owner

our.one is run by a **maintainer**: the founder, through a company of his
`[CONFIRM — which one, and whether he is the data controller personally]`.

The maintainer is bound by **a public contract**, which is also the terms
people join under. It states what the maintainer cannot do, and for each
promise what holds it today: the contract, the code or the law.

**Its promises:**

- **No sale** of our.one or of any part of it, and **no investment** for a
  return.
- **Your feed:** your people, in order, with no ranking, no advertising, and
  no selling of your data.
- **You can leave with everything:** export and deletion.
- **Every cost is public,** and money buys no reach and no say.
- **The code is open** (Apache-2.0).
- **Who holds each key is public.**
- **Changes come with notice and an exit:**
  - any change to the contract is announced 60 days ahead
    `[CONFIRM — the period]`, with the reason;
  - everyone can leave with everything before it applies.
- **The handover (§B).**

**Two of these promises cannot be amended by the maintainer at all:** no
sale, and the handover. `[CONFIRM — the founder's commitment]`

- The rest may change only with the notice and the exit above.
- Without this lock, "nothing is sold" would be a *"not for sale"* claim
  made by the back door.

### B. The handover at a membership threshold

**When the public count reaches the threshold,** the maintainer hands three
things to a not-for-profit body of the members:

- the domain `our.one`;
- the database;
- the right to replace the maintainer.

The body is founded by the members' vote, under rules published before the
threshold is reached.

**The threshold** is `[CONFIRM — 100,000 recommended; the founder offered
"100k or 1M"]`.

- **One number triggers the handover: the public count,** meaning accounts
  that exist and are not suspended. It is the same number the front page
  shows, so the goalposts can't move.
- **The vote that founds the body is separate.**
  - It is cast by verified members, one per person.
  - It is counted by someone other than the maintainer.
  - Its rules are published before the threshold `[CONFIRM]`.
- **Before the threshold,** the maintainer holds the domain, the database
  and the registrar and deploy keys (`D-0007` §E, option 3), and says so on
  every page that describes control.
- **If the threshold is never reached,** nothing is handed over and nothing
  is sold. The contract continues, the code stays open, and everyone can
  leave with their data. The site says so in those terms.

### C. The name

**our.one is the public name.** It replaces the brand OURS of `D-0004` for
the product and the network. OURS remains this repository's working name,
and `oursorg.com` its records address.

`AGENTS.md` §11 is amended in the adopting commit. `D-0004` is otherwise
unchanged.

### D. The message

**The direction:**

- **the idea:** *maintainers, not owners*;
- **the front-page headline:** *"Today it's mine. At [threshold] members, I
  give it away."* It answers the first objection before anyone raises it;
- **the live count, shown as a rank:** *"N people are in. You'd be #N+1."*
  No progress bar, because early on it would look empty;
- **the product line:** "No ads. No ranking." (not "no algorithm");
- **the story:** WhatsApp's arc from 2012 to 2025.

The draft line *"it's ours"* is dropped. A members' body gives control,
not ownership.

**Every public page that makes the promise also says:**

- what holds each promise today;
- that before the threshold it is a promise;
- what happens if the threshold is never reached.

**The status line**, wherever control is described: *"Maintained by its
founder. Handed to its members at [threshold]."* It replaces `D-0011` §B's
status line.

## Prohibited under this decision

- **Claims that the handover has happened, before it has.** Also any
  present-tense claim of ownership: *"member-owned"*, *"user-owned"*,
  *"not for sale"* as a fact rather than a promise.
- **"First" or "only"** about our.one, without a checked source.
- **A counter that counts anything other than** accounts that exist, are not
  deleted and are not suspended.
- **Deploying anything under `M-0011`.**
- **Holding any real person's data** before a release mandate, and before
  §E's conditions are met.

## Reasons

1. **Five launches found no users.** The unknown is resonance, not
   structure. Proving it before paying for structure is the right order,
   provided the promise is honest about being a promise.
2. **The promises bind the founder to every user.** They are the terms
   people join under, the code is open, and exports work. What would make
   this Ello, a sale, is forbidden.
3. **A public count turns the promise into something people can see and
   push.** Every invitation brings the threshold closer.
