# D-0001 — Publish the Authority Trace

**Status:** ADOPTED — by the founder; acted on from 30 August 2026 while marked DRAFT, status corrected under `D-0004` §C on 8 September 2026
**Class:** CONSTITUTIONAL
**Authority:** founder bootstrap
**Date drafted:** 30 August 2026
**Machine projection:** [`D-0001.yaml`](./D-0001.yaml)

## The decision

Build and publish, at `oursorg.com`, a page that lets any visitor reconstruct
the authority under which that page is running — and then reverse it.

This is the first self-hosted change. Unlike M-0000, which was written by
hand because the kernel did not yet exist, M-0001 is validated by the kernel
before any work begins.

## Why

Kernel 0.1 can refuse an invalid authority chain. Nobody outside this
repository has seen it do so, and a proof that only its author can run is not
a proof.

## What this decision authorizes

Two mandates, deliberately separate:

| Mandate | Class | What it may do |
|---|---|---|
| `M-0001` | BUILD | build the proof surface; **may not deploy it** |
| `M-0001-RELEASE` | DEPLOY | publish one verified digest to production |

The separation is the point. The mandate that builds the page cannot release
it, and the mandate that releases it cannot change what it releases. If those
were one authority, the architecture would be decoration.

## The scope denial that matters

`M-0001` is denied write access to `authority/`, `constitution/`,
`decisions/`, and `mandates/`.

The page displays those records. The mandate that builds the page cannot edit
them. An implementer who found the trace inconvenient — a failing check, an
awkward status — could otherwise fix the page by fixing the record it reads.
That is the exact failure this project exists to prevent, and it is denied by
the mandate rather than by good intentions.

## Design direction adopted

The founder directs, on 30 August 2026, that the public surface is **not** a
continuation of the OURS TODAY visual identity.

That identity — near-black ground, condensed capitals at billboard scale,
acid green and orange accents, a scarcity counter — argued the opposite of
this project's thesis. `THESIS.md` §13 disclaims "a token, blockchain, or
speculative ownership scheme"; the page looked like a mint. Volume is what a
claim uses when it has no evidence. OURS now has evidence, and a record that
shouts is less believable, not more.

The adopted direction:

- the register of an interface someone uses daily, not a campaign poster;
- a neutral UI sans for prose, and a true monospace for identifiers, digests
  and states, because those are read rather than looked at;
- near-monochrome, with status carried by typography rather than colour;
- generous whitespace and a document-like structure;
- **articles that no machine can decide are given the same visual weight as
  those that passed.** No green wall, no buried gap. This is not a styling
  preference; it is the thesis rendered.

## What this decision does not authorize

- membership, accounts, enrolment, or any form of sign-up;
- collecting any personal data, including an email address;
- analytics, tracking, or third-party scripts;
- any claim that members exist or that anything is member-owned;
- changing any record the page displays;
- moving `our.one` or altering the legacy `ourstoday` deployment.

## The counter-case

**An authority trace is credibility, not distribution.** It is what stops a
skeptic dismissing the project. It is not a reason for anyone to arrive. On
completion, OURS will hold an unimpeachable proof that nobody has a reason to
look at.

This is accepted knowingly. The gap is closed by a first Product Cell, whose
selection criteria are in `FIRST-PRODUCT.md` §15 and which is deliberately
not decided here. Publishing the proof does not constitute progress on the
demand question, and no future document may treat it as though it did.

**Second objection.** The comprehension review in `FIRST-PRODUCT.md` §14 has
still not run. Five people who do not know this project have never read the
claim. Building the page before testing whether anyone wants what it proves
repeats the predecessor project's error.

**Mitigation adopted:** the review runs against the deployed page rather than
on paper, and its results are published whether or not they are flattering.
If five readers cannot say who authorised the release and how to reverse it,
that is recorded as a `FAILED` acceptance test, not as feedback to consider.

## Reasons

1. A refusal nobody can see has not been demonstrated.
2. The build/deploy separation must be exercised on something real before it
   is claimed to hold.
3. The page is generated from the records, so it cannot drift from them —
   which makes it a projection rather than a story.
4. Static output keeps the whole thing rehostable by anyone, which is what
   makes the exit right more than a sentence.
