## What the verification found, and what is amended

The numbers are the verifiers' (H for the honesty verifier, R for the rendering
and use verifier).

### A. What a named app is kept with, and what is claimed (H1, H2)

D-0024 §B said a named app is kept with *"no key that could join it to any of
them"*, and that the day, not the moment, was kept because the moment *"would
let anyone who can read the database match a name to the waiting list by its
time."* The verifier showed this is **not true of the database**: Postgres's own
transaction id (`xmin`) of a named app's row sits between the rows the same
request wrote to `rate_events` and to `waitlist`, so anyone who can read the
database can pair them with one `select`. The row itself is as designed (a
random uuid, the words, a day), and the page never shows a word of it. What can
be pointed at is whose one is, for someone with the database.

**Amended, in place of those claims:**

- A named app is **stored** with no address, no account, no network address and
  no key to any of them: a random uuid, the words and the day in UTC.
- **Someone who can read the database could still guess whose one is** from the
  order the rows were written and from the day, most of all while few people
  have answered. So does anyone who holds the provider's history or a backup.
  The day, not the moment, makes this harder; it does not make it impossible.
- The form's hint, its refusal of an address, `/privacy`, the manifest, SPEC
  §18.23 and the receipt say so, and ask people to **leave anything about
  themselves out of it**, because it can't be changed or deleted later.

### B. The hint says what the form asks (H2)

`/privacy` said *"That is why the form asks you to leave anything about yourself
out of it"*, and the form asked no such thing. The hint now reads *"Optional, up
to 140 characters. It can't be changed or deleted later, so please leave
anything about yourself out of it."* The refusal of an address reads *"Leave
email addresses out of this one. It can't be changed or deleted later, and we
don't need it."*

### C. The count says "answers" (H3)

D-0024 §C said the count is of *"apps named"* and, in the same section, that it
*"counts what people typed, not different apps."* The page claimed the first.
It now says **"1 answer so far."** and **"N answers so far."**, which is what is
counted. (This resolves, for now, the founder's open question 5 of P-0017 in the
accurate direction; the founder may choose otherwise.)

### D. Choices the build made that D-0024 did not mark, or did not mention (H10)

D-0024's preface says every choice the notes left open is marked *"(agent's
choice)"*; three were not. **They are agent's choices, and they are the
founder's to confirm:**

1. **One form on the page.** The notes say "one form"; the build reads that as
   exactly one, so the feed's panel under *A first project* carries no form and
   points to the first.
2. **The link card's look and words:** the headline on the paper at 1200 × 630,
   with the site's wordmark and *"It starts with a friends feed."* under it; the
   page's own title and description for its card (R5), and no card text of the
   card's own.
3. **An email address in a need is refused,** at the need's own field, in the
   spellings the build reads (a space beside the @, a full-width or small @, a
   full-width or ideographic dot; H7). The notes say nothing of it; it decides
   who may answer and in what words, and it is not a general filter: it can't
   tell other things about a person from an app's name.

### E. What a need may hold (H8)

Every control character (C0 and C1) and run of white space becomes a single
space, the bidi controls are dropped, and a need of nothing a reader could see
(zero-width, filler, soft-hyphen and the like) is kept as nothing at all.

### F. The first screen's line (H9)

D-0024 §A called the line *"these are the last two sentences of the front door's
existing description"*. They are **a rephrasing** of them: `DOOR_LEDE` ends
*"Starting with a friends feed. Building toward much more."*, and the line reads
*"It starts with a friends feed. Building toward much more."*, which turns a
plan into a statement of fact the notes themselves used. P-0017 proposed the one
sentence *"It starts with a friends feed."*; the build keeps the second, which
is `DOOR_LEDE`'s own. Both are the founder's to choose between.

### G. The form without JavaScript, and the referrer policy (H5, R4)

The build had given `useActionState` a client closure instead of the server
action, so the join form, on the front door **and on /feed**, no longer posted
without JavaScript. That broke what M-0021 says it leaves alone. The form is
the server action again, as it was, and a refusal carries what was typed
(`values`) from the action itself, so the fields are still put back.

The verifier also found that a form without JavaScript had **already** failed
before M-0021, with a 500: the root layout's `<meta name="referrer"
content="no-referrer">` makes the browser send `Origin: null` on a native form
post, which Next refuses, and `next.config.ts` had chosen `Referrer-Policy:
same-origin` for exactly that reason. The meta overrode the header. **It now
says `same-origin`**; another site still gets no referrer. This is a change to a
root-layout setting that M-0021 did not set out to touch, made because the
form could not otherwise be shown to work.

### H. Keyboard, the answer, the 140 (R2, R3, R7)

- The one answer is drawn **above** the fields, where the person was, and not
  under the button.
- The button is `aria-disabled` while the form is sent, not `disabled`, so
  keyboard focus stays on it.
- The question's field has no `maxlength`: a longer need is refused at the
  field, in words, and the hint states the 140.

### I. The first screen's size (R1)

The headline is smaller (at most 80px; 76px was the stylesheet's size on narrower
screens already) and the first screen's margins are tighter, so that at 1440 ×
900 the form's button ends within the window. **At smaller windows the form is
still below the first screen** (1366 × 768, 1280 × 720, and every phone): the
order D-0024 §A sets leaves the form last, after the count, the pledge and the
status line, and only a different layout could change that.

### J. The card draws the site's wordmark (R6)

`public/card.png` is drawn again with *our*, a rust full stop, *one*, as the
header draws it.

### K. The records say which commits were checked (H4)

The build receipt said `pnpm ours check M-0021 --changed` was run *"before each
commit of the build"*. It was run on the working tree before the build commits
(`30f1cc8`, `943a90c`), and `--changed` evaluates only what is uncommitted, so
it says nothing about a commit once made. The records commits (`61ccbbe`,
`c09a7b2`, `c9ac8bc`) were records-only and outside M-0021's scope by design. The
receipt now lists them apart and says exactly that.

## Rule 6 of `apps/web/AGENTS.md`, and a named app (H6)

`apps/web/AGENTS.md` rule 6 reads *"People can leave. Keep export and deletion
working for everything in `data.collects`."* A named app is declared in
`data.collects`, as rule 2 requires, **and cannot be exported or deleted by the
person who answered, because nothing stored with it says whose it is.** The
manifest says so in `data.export` and `data.delete`, and `/privacy` says it. The
kit's check passes only because *"Do export and deletion work?"* is a question
it leaves to a person.

That is a departure from rule 6, **made by the build, not decided by anyone**.
The rule says: *"If a task would break one, stop and say which."* The task was
finished rather than stopped, and the departure is recorded here, in the build
receipt and in the final report. **The founder decides, by record, which of
these it is:**

1. the rule is amended for data that is stored with nothing that identifies
   it, and a named app stays as it is;
2. the question is dropped from the form, and `Named apps` leaves
   `data.collects`;
3. a named app gets a way to be deleted: for example a one-time code shown once
   after the answer, of which only a hash is kept. That would change the one
   answer's shape, and the hash would be a key to the need (though not to the
   address).

Until the founder decides, the question is asked, as D-0024 says.

## Open, and the founder's

1. **Rule 6** (above): amend the rule, drop the question, or give a named app a
   deletion code.
2. **Whether to reduce what a database reader can infer** (§A): for example by
   rewriting the table in one transaction, in random order, on a schedule or on
   every insert, so that its rows share a transaction id and carry no order. It
   would not help while few people have answered, and it does not reach the
   provider's history. Until the founder decides, the claim is as §A states it.
3. **Whether the count says "answers"** (§C) or something else.
4. **Which of the first screen's lines to use** (§F), and the other choices in §D.
5. **The first screen below the fold on a small window** (§I): whether a
   different layout is wanted.
6. **How many seats to open** when this goes out, and **whether the named apps
   are ever published**: as in D-0024, and still the founder's.
