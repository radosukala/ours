## How to read the articles

Every article declares how it is actually held today, with the classes the
constitution uses: `ENFORCED` · `CHECKED` · `STRUCTURAL` · `INTERPRETED` ·
`DECLARED`. No article may be untagged.

## Article C-PURPOSE — Share four machines fairly among the people who keep the workshop

The tool exists to let the people who keep the workshop share its four
machines fairly. Changing the purpose needs two thirds of members.

**Class:** `STRUCTURAL` — the charter names it · `INTERPRETED` — whether a
change serves it is the members' judgment

## Article C-PARTICIPATION — Who belongs, and who decides

Anyone who pays the workshop fee has standing. Guests book through a
member. The register of members is private and kept by the coordinator;
the count is public. A decision by vote is the members' decision; a
decision delegated to a role is made by the person standing names in it,
and by nobody else.

**Class:** `ENFORCED` — the kernel refuses a decision by an actor without
standing · `INTERPRETED` — who has paid is the coordinator's to know

## Article C-DECISIONS — By what procedure

Amendments to this arrangement: two thirds of members. Rules of the tool
and improvements to it: a majority of those who respond. Routine
operation: the coordinator, receipted. Emergencies — a machine unsafe, a
breach — the operator, receipted, reviewed at the next vote.

**Class:** `ENFORCED` — the kernel checks every vote's tally against this
article's threshold for its class, and every delegated decision against
standing

## Article C-MONEY — What it costs and who pays

Two euros a month from each member into the group's account. Anyone can
see the ledger. Decisions about money need two thirds.

**Class:** `DECLARED` — no ledger exists in this fixture; the account is
described, not held

## Article C-PRIVACY — What is seen, and by whom

Bookings are visible to members. Nothing else is collected. What the tool
may reach and disclose is in the specification's contract. The legal data
controller is not yet named.

**Class:** `STRUCTURAL` — the contract lists the fields · `DECLARED` — the
controller

## Article C-CONTINUITY — If the operator leaves

A second person can run the tool from the records alone. This is rehearsed
once a year. If the operator leaves tomorrow, the records are enough; the
name and the address are meant to be held for the group and today sit in
the founding team's account — an open step.

**Class:** `DECLARED` — the rehearsal is described here and not yet
receipted in this fixture · `INTERPRETED` — whether the records were enough

## The steps to community control

1. founding arrangement adopted — done
2. every record public and checkable — done
3. operator replaceable, rehearsed — done, in the story; not receipted here
4. data controller named — open
5. name and address held for the group — open
6. a decision the founding team cannot override — open

Three of six remain. Until they are done, the tool says *operated by the
founding team* at the top, and nothing here calls it community-controlled.
