# OURS — connections first

> **We stay. Apps come and go.**

**Status: PROPOSED.** Product direction and implementation plan for a coding agent. Written at the founder's explicit request in the current conversation. The founder endorsed the photo-sharing entrance, portable human connections, replaceable applications, and the movement sentence above. The technical design, sequencing, suggested defaults, and acceptance criteria below are agent proposals, not independently adopted rules.

This document authorises no production build, deployment, membership issuance, legal arrangement, migration, or external message. It is the requested planning artifact. Resolve implementation authority under [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), preserving accepted conversational direction and making outstanding decisions concrete. Do not treat completion of this document as completion of the product.

## 1. The product we are trying to create

**OURS is a place for relationships and shared life to persist while the software serving them can change.**

The first application helps people collect photos from something they did together: a dinner, trip, party, or recurring gathering. The enduring product is the people's ability to keep connecting and sharing on their own terms, without needing a particular application or supplier to survive.

A participant should be able to understand the value through an ordinary experience:

> Our photos are here. Our people are here. We can change the app without starting again.

The founder's diagnosis is that social products increasingly mediate which connections people see through objectives they did not choose. Treat that as motivation for a concrete alternative, not as a sourced historical claim about every platform. Public claims about competitors require specific current evidence. Likewise, do not claim code is literally free, infrastructure has no value, or member ownership guarantees commercial competitors will disappear.

The working economic hypothesis is narrower: as software becomes easier to produce, relationships, trust, and organised demand can persist across replaceable implementations. Builders can serve an existing group with a known need. Growth comes from useful invitations and repeat use, not from requiring everyone to learn the thesis.

### What changed from the button

The button demonstrated a voting mechanism. This product must deliver something people already want and make continuity a real capability. Voting is available where a collective decision is needed; it is not the main activity. Do not revive colour voting, ownership fractions, a constitutional tutorial, or a large proposal dashboard as the entrance.

### What changed from the bridge proposal

Keep the insight that people arrive through existing relationships. Begin with a self-service occasion and photo space rather than a bespoke founding consultancy for an association. Existing legal entities may be useful pilot partners, but creating an entity per dinner or manually writing a constitution per group is not the intended consumer flow.

This is a product proposal within the enduring-layer direction in [THE-LAYER.md](../foundation/THE-LAYER.md), not an automatic amendment of its canonical scope or opening conditions.

## 2. The sentence means something testable

“We stay” means identity, consented relationships, permitted records, membership, privacy choices, and the ability to continue using the space can survive replacement of a compatible application or authorised operator.

It does not mean people cannot leave. It does not mean we own another person, can force them to remain connected, or can export everything they once shared into a new context.

“Apps come and go” means the application is a replaceable client of a stable service contract. A new client cannot redefine the community's rights, access scope, data meaning, or audience just by rendering another interface.

Changing a theme is not application replacement. A second independent client reading and writing the same governed records proves more. A rehearsed transition to an independently controlled operator proves another capability. Legal ownership and enforceable asset control require their own evidence. Report each separately.

## 3. Scope of the initial product

### Included in the first useful slice

- A browser-based private space for a shared occasion.
- Inviting someone by a revocable, limited invitation; deliberate acceptance and an appropriate admission policy.
- Uploading images and a short caption; browsing eligible photos in a predictable order.
- Clear identification of who can see a contribution before it is shared.
- Mutual connections between consenting participants, separate from simply belonging to the same occasion.
- A small People view in the OURS shell, outside the photo client's authority.
- Recoverable identity, withdrawal/disconnection/blocking, and narrowly scoped export/deletion behaviour.
- A replacement photo client using the same service contract and permitted records.
- A subsequent independently operated handover drill before claiming operator replaceability.

### Deferred

Public discovery, recommendation engines, follower-count status, advertising, direct messaging, video processing, face recognition, contact-book import, automatic cross-platform graph import, arbitrary third-party code, a general marketplace, and an AI app factory.

Start with invited adult pilot participants as a proposed risk-reducing boundary; the exact admission and content policy is a human decision before opening. This is not a claim that every image depicts an adult or that an adults-only rule solves photo consent.

## 4. The user journey

### Arrival through the main page

Proposed copy:

> Your friends aren't a feature.
>
> A place for your people, with apps you can replace.
>
> **Start something together**
>
> See how changing the app works

The movement sentence can appear quietly: “We stay. Apps come and go.” Keep OURS as the brand. Domain and legal entity are separate decisions; this plan grants no domain action.

A demonstration may use explicitly labelled sample data. It must not pretend the operator has changed when only the page changed. A visitor should be able to understand the premise without opening governance records.

### Create an occasion

A creator names the occasion, sees the chosen admission/privacy defaults, and adds a photo. Only request information needed for participation and recovery. Show costs or limits before commitment, not after people have entrusted their photos.

The creator's administrative duties do not silently award superior constitutional standing. The opening arrangement names practical moderation and invitation powers and how to challenge or replace their holder.

### Invite existing people

The person shares a link in the channel the group already uses. No automated contact scraping or agent-sent invitations.

A private invitation page can show a neutral occasion title and inviter-approved preview. Private media is not readable before the recipient obtains the required permission. An invite link being easy to share does not make it a public album URL. Forwarded links, previews, crawlers, expiry, redemption limits, and admission revocation need deliberate handling.

Proposed eventual invitation: “Put your photos here. This one belongs to us.” Use this only once the issued rights justify it. Until then, invite honestly into a clearly described pilot without an ownership claim.

### Contribute and return

A recipient joins deliberately, sees the photos they are entitled to see, adds their own, and can return without being required to vote or recruit anyone.

Make the initial album useful immediately. Avoid a blank profile, a constitution questionnaire, a demand counter, or a global empty feed. Loading, interrupted uploads and retries must give honest status.

### Keep a connection

Being in an album together does not automatically make participants friends across OURS.

Offer a simple “Stay connected” action. The other person can accept, decline, ignore, later disconnect, or block. A shared occasion can end without destroying an independently accepted connection.

The People view can lead to shared spaces and a deliberate invitation to another occasion. A connection grants no blanket access to someone's past/future media, other circles, activity or contact details. Notifications and future audiences have their own consent.

### Change the application

A participant can preview the other approved client using their own existing permissions. This is a personal presentation choice, not a vote about everyone else's interface.

Recommended first clients:

- **Journal:** a chronological stream of photos and captions.
- **Album:** an occasion-organised gallery of the same eligible photos, with the same core contribution actions.

Both must support the pinned core behaviours; neither may lose captions, identity attribution, permission semantics, deletion state or export ability. Any difference beyond presentation/accessibility must be explicit.

Choosing a default for the group follows its adopted procedure. Replacing an application cannot broaden its data contract. A new app with new permissions needs fresh authorisation, not an invisible compatibility exception.

### Change the operator

The group authorises a successor under the actual membership and custody arrangement. The transition preserves permitted data and agreed behaviour, rotates/revokes relevant access, and directs people to the successor through an address/identity mechanism not captive to the old operator.

Proposed completion copy, usable only after a real verified transition:

> The app changed. We're still here.

Name remaining limits. Old operators may have backups, cached data or prior legitimate access. Revocation prevents future authorised access; it does not remotely erase screenshots or prove deletion of a malicious copy.

## 5. The relationship contract

The core records are relationships between people with continuing agency, not a saleable list of leads.

| Concept | Meaning | What it does not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Account authentication | A credential can act for a stable subject | A verified unique human or issued membership |
| Mutual connection | Both endpoints accepted an ongoing connection in the stated scope | Content access, an address-book disclosure, or a permanent obligation |
| Space membership | A participant is admitted to a particular space | Friendship with every member or access to unrelated spaces |
| Governance standing | A defined right under the operative arrangement | Extra power from popularity, uploading or inviting |
| Audience grant | A particular subject/group may read a record under a policy | Permission to redistribute it or move it into a wider context |
| Application grant | A client may perform named operations for a subject and space | Ownership of the people or a reusable global data credential |

### Proposed connection state transitions

`absent → requested → accepted → disconnected`

A request may also become `declined`, `cancelled`, or `expired`. Blocking is a private enforcement state with its own lifecycle. Either endpoint can withdraw its participation without the other's permission. Reconnection needs renewed consent; a restored backup or application switch cannot resurrect a revoked connection.

Pending invitations, declined requests, block state and hidden memberships must not be exposed through graph traversal or “people you may know.” No public graph or cross-space search is included initially.

### Content access after relationships change

Separate connection state from content access. Disconnecting does not silently delete authored media or transfer ownership. Membership removal and blocking can affect current reads under the adopted policy, including direct media URLs and cached projections.

The effect of blocking within a shared space needs an explicit, visible design. Proposed baseline: no direct contact or new invitations, hide the blocked party in the blocker’s ordinary view, and provide clear information about the limits of shared-space visibility. Do not claim mutual invisibility if the implementation only hides an interface element. The exact policy and appeal path are opening decisions.

### Portability with consent

Preserve the provenance, scope, and current validity of mutual acceptance. A moved identity or provider must not turn one person's export into permission to disclose another person's private endpoint or move their account.

Distinguish an authorised successor operator for the same space from a new recipient, purpose or audience. The latter may require additional consent; the code must not decide they are equivalent. Exports include only the requester's permitted slice. Describe whether a connection is active, awaiting reconfirmation, or unavailable after a transition.

For the initial release, fully support connection continuity across approved applications on the same layer. Promise cross-operator or cross-host continuity only for the migration cases actually implemented and rehearsed. Uncooperative-provider recovery is a separate test from a friendly handover.

## 6. Make the original social promise concrete

Proposed service behaviour:

- The photo views display eligible shared contributions in a documented chronological order, with stable ordering and pagination.
- Do not suppress eligible records to maximise time spent, or insert unrelated recommended content into the default view.
- A member can find eligible shared records even if a notification was not delivered. Visibility, ordering, and notification delivery are distinct.
- Respect deletions, blocks, safety restrictions, audience grants and a person's chosen filters. Where appropriate, explain a removed item without disclosing protected details.
- Personal notification settings remain personal. Do not vote someone into receiving notifications or reveal that they silenced a group.
- Explain the end of the current eligible collection plainly. Do not manufacture endless material or urgency to keep the session alive.

A client could still hide or misrepresent data it can read. Therefore test the complete approved clients against the service contract, retain a trusted reference view/export in the OURS shell, and make client choice meaningful. An API cannot by itself prove that every client rendered its response honestly.

Measure fulfilled sharing and repeat usefulness. Avoid a target based solely on time spent or reaction volume. These are product proposals to be explicitly adopted before being described as constraints that hold.

## 7. Proposed architecture

```text
Person-controlled credentials and recovery
                 │
                 ▼
OURS identity, connections, membership and audience records
                 │
       permission-scoped service API
          ┌──────┴──────┐
          ▼             ▼
     Journal client  Album client

Storage, operating duties, and continuity mechanisms
are replaceable under a separately authorised transition.
```

### Minimum durable record families

Names are illustrative, not a mandated public protocol:

- `Subject`: stable identifier, lifecycle and permitted public profile; credentials/recovery stored separately.
- `Connection`: endpoints, acceptance provenance/scope, state, version and revocation metadata with restricted visibility.
- `Space` and `Membership`: identity, admission rules, roles, standing references and lifecycle.
- `Invitation`: purpose, intended scope, expiry, use limits, revocation and acceptance evidence; no raw tokens in logs.
- `Media` and `Contribution`: opaque identifiers, uploader, caption, occasion, audience policy, version, deletion state; private media storage references.
- `ApplicationGrant`: subject/space/operations, contract version, audience and expiry/revocation as applicable.
- `Decision` and `ReleaseReference`: reuse compatible existing authority machinery; retain human text beside projections.
- `Transition`: source/destination authority, snapshot version, permission migration, verification and cutover/rollback outcome.

An application holds no sole identity registry or source of truth for the social graph. A UI/session login ID must not become the durable subject identity. One stable internal identity does not require publishing globally correlatable identifiers to every app or group.

### API boundaries

Specify concrete operations for invitation acceptance, relationship request/accept/withdraw, eligible contribution listing, scoped upload, deletion, capability revocation, export and transition. Enforce permissions server-side on reads, writes, media retrieval and exports. UI hiding is insufficient.

Credentials are short-lived/scoped as appropriate and revocable. Avoid placing app/provider secrets in bundles or exposing recovery information to photo clients. Define consistency and stale-write handling for membership, audience changes, revocation, uploads, and transition cutover.

### Photos are sensitive records

Private object storage, permission-checked delivery, bounded uploads, safe image processing, and a decided metadata policy are part of the core. Proposed default: strip location metadata from delivered derivatives and clearly decide whether originals are retained. Do not silently expose originals through public URLs, EXIF, share cards, access logs, backups or thumbnails.

Set storage/size/retention limits and visible funding terms. Plan for malicious uploads, third-party subjects in photographs, takedown requests, reporting and bounded moderation. Do not claim end-to-end encryption unless designed and verified; its key recovery and operator-transition implications need explicit treatment.

### Provider and protocol choice

Inspect the current code and authorised scope before selecting vendors. Prefer a simple transactional store and private media storage where suitable, behind narrow interfaces and an exportable schema. This is not an instruction to introduce a distributed database, blockchain, public social graph or custom federation protocol.

Evaluate existing identity/portability systems against this private, mutual-connection use case. Public-network portability should not be assumed to solve private graph consent, member governance, or media custody. Previous research references are listed below; recheck their current capabilities before making a dependency choice.

## 8. Prove different kinds of replacement separately

| Capability | Required demonstration | Insufficient evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Application replacement | Independently implemented client conforms to the same contract; eligible content and connections remain usable; old grant can be revoked | Theme or layout toggle in one component |
| Infrastructure migration | Restore the permitted state in a clean environment, including media and privacy state; reads/writes continue with known cutover semantics | A downloadable JSON file |
| Operator replacement | An independently controlled successor can operate, recover and deploy under valid authority; prior credentials no longer authorise operations; continuity entry points remain available | A second account controlled by the same founder |
| Ownership | Issued rights, asset/custody duties, replacement/exit procedure and residual powers correspond to actual arrangements | Open source, a poll, a declaration, or an “owner” badge |

Application replacement can be demonstrated with fixtures before ownership exists. Real-person operation and ownership claims have additional opening conditions. Never collapse these statuses into a single success label.

## 9. Operator handover protocol to specify and rehearse

1. Validate the authorising decision, successor, allowed data scope, affected-participant notice/consent requirements and funding.
2. Verify the successor satisfies the same pinned service contract and permitted infrastructure/data-processing terms.
3. Produce a consistent snapshot of permitted data, private media, identities, consent states, revocations, tombstones and relevant records. Protect secrets and recovery material separately.
4. Restore to the successor and compare meaning and permissions, not just row counts or byte digests. Test previously denied actions remain denied.
5. Use a defined write barrier or versioned catch-up to prevent concurrent updates being lost. Preserve any withdrawal or deletion arriving during the move.
6. Move the continuity entry point and signing/credential authority as authorised. Revoke old capabilities and infrastructure access. Require reauthentication where necessary rather than migrating raw session secrets casually.
7. Verify reads, contributions, recovery and connection state after cutover. Ensure participants can reach the successor without asking the removed operator for permission.
8. Retire old resources under the agreed retention/deletion duties. Report backup expiry and residual copy risk.
9. Publish a privacy-safe transition receipt and an intelligible status. If verification fails, follow the agreed recovery procedure; rollback cannot resurrect a block, deletion or revoked permission.

A friendly migration rehearsal is an initial milestone. A separate outage/refusal drill must establish what recovery is possible if the previous operator is unavailable or unwilling. Do not advertise that property based on the friendly case.

## 10. Who decides what

| Action | Proposed authority |
|---|---|
| Accept/disconnect/block a relationship | The affected person; no group vote to override their withdrawal |
| Choose a personal compatible view or mute notifications | The person |
| Share their contribution with a stated audience | The author within the space's adopted policy and others' rights |
| Admit participants, moderate shared content | Named roles under the adopted procedure, with review/appeal |
| Change group defaults or adopt a new service | Eligible members through the specified procedure |
| Broaden data access, replace an operator, alter governing rights | The applicable higher authority/procedure; not a routine UI action |
| Build a bounded feature | A builder under a valid mandate; no automatic governance power |

Proposals express needs; endorsements do not grant standing. A build vote pins the offer, permissions, cost, willing builder, acceptance conditions and amendment path before execution. Apply the existing suggestion → buildable offer → decision → mandate → verified release loop when a real need arises; do not put its machinery on the first screen.

## 11. Ownership and opening conditions

The current [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) still labels the project founder bootstrap with legal membership and member ownership not issued. The local [D-0007 YAML](../decisions/D-0007.yaml) inspected for this plan is `DRAFT`. This plan changes neither.

Before real private data is entrusted to the layer or public ownership is asserted, resolve the applicable opening requirements, including:

- actual member rights and obligations, joining/leaving and the relationship between root membership and a space;
- who controls the domain/address, identities/recovery, repository, infrastructure, media and funds;
- the named operator/controller and successor appointment, removal, refusal and recovery procedures;
- legal/operational custody, permitted processing and transfer, retention, notices and any required consent;
- recoverable identity and membership admission; authentication must not be mistaken for unique personhood;
- baseline safety/moderation, appeals, reporting, privacy and block behaviour;
- licence, operating budget, funding and service-continuity limits;
- the brief opening deal's explicit relationship to the existing constitution.

Aim for a reusable arrangement that lets ordinary participants start spaces without bespoke incorporation. That is a design objective, not a legal conclusion. Resolve it with a human and qualified advice. Do not invent joint equity, dividable ownership fractions or a token.

Keep the difference between personal records, bilateral connections, shared assets, operating authority and legal ownership visible in the design. “Owning our connections” means meaningful control and continuity, not the ability to sell or command the other endpoint.

## 12. Implementation phases and deliverables

Each phase needs applicable implementation authority; later phases are not authorised by finishing an earlier one. Use fictional data until the real-data opening requirements are met. Unknown policy choices should become a short, concrete decision proposal, while independent authorised work continues.

### Phase A — inspect and pin the first slice

Inspect current branches and actual implementation locations. Locate any deployed button source rather than assuming it is in this checkout. Read the existing kernel, schemas, CLI, verifier, community fixtures, relevant mandates and unresolved decisions. Produce a short reuse/gap map and propose the minimum implementation mandate; do not restart the repository or widen an unrelated fixture mandate.

Pin a small service specification covering the journey, record meanings, permissions, connection states and failure behaviour. State which choices are accepted, proposed, or awaiting human decision. Do not assign guessed mandate IDs as if issued.

**Deliverable:** scoped plan/mandate proposal and testable contract with the smallest useful photo loop.

### Phase B — user-perspective prototype

Show creator and invitee paths, a useful album with fictional data, a deliberate connection request, acceptance, disconnection/blocking, personal application preview and a clearly simulated transition.

Retain the warmth and simplicity of the prior approved visual direction. Lead with people and shared photos. Keep admin/verification one step deeper. Desktop, mobile, accessible controls, honest loading/error states and explicit fixture labels are required for the relevant paths.

**Deliverable:** an inspectable prototype that demonstrates usefulness and ownership-related actions without claiming they exist in production.

### Phase C — minimal private layer and photo client

Implement recoverable identity, admission, scoped media, connections, audience rules, predictable listing, revocation and export under a pinned contract. Use server-authoritative operations, transaction/retry rules and private storage. Start with synthetic users; real participants only after the applicable gates.

**Deliverable:** a working photo loop and private connection state, with meaningful denial tests and a truthful capability report.

### Phase D — independent application replacement

Build the alternate client against the same contract. It must not depend on the first client's private state or undocumented database writes. A participant can use the replacement; changes, deletions and disconnections are reflected correctly. Revoke the first client's grant and verify it loses access while the replacement remains usable.

**Deliverable:** conformance evidence and a recorded application switch. Label this application replacement, not operator independence or issued ownership.

### Phase E — operator continuity

Bring up the successor under independently held control with the applicable authority, rehearse the protocol above and then test the defined outage/refusal case. Account for address and identity continuity, recovery material, deletion state, storage costs and residual copies.

**Deliverable:** transition evidence, failure/recovery report and a precise statement of what the members can now exercise.

### Phase F — invited real-world pilot

Use actual shared occasions after opening conditions are satisfied. Observe contribution, retrieval, repeat use and whether people can initiate another space without founder assistance. Do not manufacture participants or cast production votes to make a demo look populated.

**Deliverable:** privacy-respecting observations of usefulness and independently understood continuity, with a keep/change/stop recommendation.

### Phase G — a second useful service when demanded

When an actual group asks for a useful capability beyond photos, prepare a bounded offer. Reuse membership and consented connections without sharing private records by default. If adopted, build and release through the existing authority chain.

**Deliverable:** evidence that organised demand can commission replaceable software. A marketplace or general app factory is deferred until repeated real needs justify it.

## 13. Acceptance scenarios

These specify expected behaviour for later implementation; they are not test results.

### Human connection and consent

- Joining an occasion does not create mutual connections automatically.
- A request cannot self-approve; replaying acceptance is idempotent; withdrawal wins over a stale acceptance.
- Either endpoint can disconnect. A group administrator cannot reconnect them.
- Blocking prevents the promised interactions across both clients, direct API calls and delivery paths; private block state does not leak.
- A new space/client does not reveal another space's members, graph or media.
- Recovering an identity retains legitimate standing without duplicating the person or restoring revoked consent.

### Shared photos and ordering

- An unauthorised visitor, forwarded invitation, preview crawler or expired credential cannot read private media.
- A member sees the eligible collection through stable pagination; empty/loading/deleted states are distinguishable.
- Retried uploads do not duplicate contributions; failed uploads do not appear successful.
- Delivered images follow the chosen metadata policy. Public previews, logs and exports do not leak originals or private identifiers.
- Revoked audience grants take effect at media delivery and projections under the stated cache policy, not only in visible lists.

### Replacement and independence

- The alternate client preserves record meaning, eligible audiences and core behaviours; it is not a skin of the first client.
- Disconnection, deletion, block state and revocation survive application switch, export/restore and cutover.
- A removed client/operator's stale credentials cannot read or write after the declared revocation point.
- A revoked consent arriving during migration is not lost; rollback does not revive access.
- The successor can operate under independent control and participants can find it through the designed continuity mechanism.
- A private personal export cannot become an unrestricted copy of other people's relationships or media.
- A proposed new client with a broader contract is refused until the required approval exists.

### Authority and truthful reporting

- Unadopted prerequisites, out-of-scope work, mismatched artifacts and unauthorised release attempts are refused by the actual named checks.
- A simulation, file export, friendly migration, independent operator replacement and issued ownership have distinct statuses and evidence.
- Public statements do not infer zero-cost operation, guaranteed growth or non-bypassability from a passing demo.

Run relevant existing repository checks when their code changes, plus the scoped product tests. Do not run the whole authority suite merely to label a Markdown plan tested. Agree a stopping point: once the scoped checks pass, record the result and outstanding decisions rather than expanding into unrelated machinery.

## 14. Growth and economic test

Proposed loop:

**Shared occasion → useful shared space → voluntary invitations → contributions → return visit → another occasion or group.**

An invitation serves the recipient's immediate interest in their group's photos. Starting another space is available after someone has experienced value. Notifications require their own permission. Referrals, uploading volume, support, money and seniority never silently increase governance weight.

Observe whether people contribute because they want the shared result, retrieve it later, return for a further occasion, start spaces without personal assistance, and can explain what they retain when software changes. Define numerical pilot success/stop thresholds and a spending/time cap with the founder before recruitment; this plan does not fabricate benchmarks.

Measure acquisition and retention separately from the institutional test. A popular album can still fail operator replacement. A technically portable graph can still fail to interest people. Feedback about the story is not evidence of either.

The ambition is self-service formation and distribution that can repeat without bespoke founder work. Storage, reliability, support, moderation, recovery and accountable operations require funding. Propose visible pilot subsidies and eventual sustainable contributions/fees with no surprise billing. Equal standing does not require unpaid maintenance. Do not build payments before the chosen funding arrangement needs them.
