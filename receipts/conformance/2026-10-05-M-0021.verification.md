## The stopping rule, declared before it starts

This is the rule M-0020 used, as M-0021 asks.

1. **Two verifiers that built none of it,** each in its own worktree outside
   the main checkout (its `apps/web/.env.local` may hold real keys, D-0021
   §F):
   - **honesty:** every sentence and label M-0021 added or changed, and every
     claim the records make about it: the first screen's words, the form's
     question and its hint, the refusals' words, the two count lines, the
     member's panel line, the link card's title, description and alt, `SPEC.md`
     §18.23, the privacy page's and the kit manifest's *Named apps*, the build
     receipt, and P-0017, D-0024 and M-0021 themselves, including what they
     say about being a second draft after a lost build. They are checked
     against M-0021's acceptance criteria one by one, AGENTS.md (§5 on
     prohibited actions, §6, §9 and §10 on checkable claims) and what the code
     does. In particular, by running the requests and reading every table, the
     outbox and the log: that nothing stored with a named app can find whose it
     is; that the answer and the work are the same for every address; that
     every refusal keeps nothing; that a need which can't be kept never
     refuses a seat; that `0003_needs` applies on a fresh database and its down
     migration undoes it; and that code which reaches a database before the
     migration asks nothing it can't keep. The feed's verified words stay
     unchanged;
   - **rendering and use:** the front door as a visitor and as a member, in a
     browser, from a production build, at 375, 820, 1000 and 1440px, light and
     dark, and at 320px for overflow. By keyboard alone: the whole form, a
     refusal at each field (what is announced, where focus goes, what was
     kept), the hint's tie to its field. The contrast of the new elements. The
     feed's panel and its count. The head tags of every public page and the
     card's image, as served. The public pages with the database down, and with
     `APP_URL` unset in production.
2. **Their tests are committed as written,** before any fix: each finding is a
   `DEFECT` test that fails on the verified commit; each sound check a `closed`
   test that passes.
3. **Every finding is fixed or recorded.** A fixed `DEFECT` test is renamed
   *"fixed ("*; a test adapted to a fix says *"Changed after…"* and why.
4. **One re-check** by an agent that built and verified none of it.
5. **Every re-check finding is fixed or recorded,** and there is no further
   round.

**What the founder asks for is not a finding.** The screens went to the

## The re-check is waived

The stopping rule's item 4, one re-check by an agent that built and verified
none of it, **was not run.** The founder, after the build had cost more than
they wanted, asked for it to be finished cheaply, and a third agent is the
costly part. The fixes were checked by the verifiers' own tests and one full
run of the suite (the build receipt gives the numbers), and by no one who did
not build them. **There was therefore no re-check finding, and no claim here
that a re-check found nothing.** The founder may ask for it before any deploy;
it would be the next step of the rule, and its findings are fixed or recorded
with no further round.

The founder's instruction to "commit to main" was not a finding, and the
founder then objected to a publish: **nothing is on `main`.**
