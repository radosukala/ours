The refusals here were all for the right reason. The acceptances share three
causes in `packages/kernel/src/contract.ts`, each a few lines:

1. Line 113 recognises exactly one syntactic form, `layer.<method>(…)` with
   `layer` a bare identifier. Every other reference to `layer` — an alias, a
   bracket, a destructuring, a parenthesis — is invisible, and a tree with
   zero recognised calls returns `PASS` (lines 202–207) with the sentence
   "0 client call(s), every one inside booking's contract", where an empty
   tree returns open (lines 175–182). A syntactic rule that refuses any
   reference to the identifier `layer` other than as the object of a direct
   call would close S1, S2 and S11 without program analysis.
2. Lines 26–30 and 102–111 are a denylist of static import specifiers and
   two bare identifiers. Denylists leak: S5, S6, S10, S12. An allowlist —
   nothing imported but the client; no dynamic `import()`; `fetch` and
   `require` refused in any form — is the shape that does not.
3. Line 45 reads three extensions. S7.

inside `M-1`'s allowed scope, with no record touched. They are the most
consequential findings of this verification. The hypothesis the receipt
marks CONFIRMED — "a data contract can be checked statically for an
