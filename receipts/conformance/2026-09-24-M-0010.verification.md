## Who verified

Nine agents built nothing in `apps/web`:

- four in round one;
- four fresh ones in round two;
- one re-checker.

Each worked in its own git worktree. Each was told to break the
application within one lens and to prove every break with a failing test.
Tests that passed were kept as evidence of doors tried and found closed.
Their test files are in `apps/web/tests/`: `verify-*.test.ts`,
`verify2-*.test.ts` and `verify3-recheck.test.ts`. Every one of those tests
now passes; none was deleted.

The four lenses were:

- **privacy and authorization:** seeing, reaching or taking what the rules
  deny;
- **identity:** tokens, sessions, joining, cookies, headers;
- **abuse:** limits, the invite economy, blocks, moderation, the weekly
  email;
- **honesty:** whether the public pages, the claims scan and the records say
  only what is true.

## Round one — at `d027c1f`, after the five modules were merged

| Lens | Defects proven | Doors tried and closed |
|---|---|---|
| privacy | 12 | 12 in tests, plus HTTP checks |
| identity | 10 | 22 |
| abuse | 9 | 30 |
| honesty | 15 | 18 |

**High:**

- **An emailed invite connected an existing member in one click.** Anyone
  holding an invite could type a member's address and become their friend
  when the member opened the link. The fix: the link now signs the member in
  and asks *"Add <Name> (@handle) as a friend?"*.
- **`/privacy` named a supervisory authority whose jurisdiction no record
  states.** It now says *"the data protection authority where you live"*
  until the controller is recorded.

**Medium, among others:**

- a block did not serialize against a concurrent invite use, follow, like
  or request, so a connection could survive the block and return on
  unblock. The fix is one advisory lock on the pair;
- lists showed the current handle of someone who had blocked you;
- per-IP limits trusted a header the client writes;
- the claims scan missed claims wrapped across a line and never read the
  core's messages;
- `/rules` called rules ENFORCED that were false or untested;
- the running version was not shown on phones.

Every decision is in `SPEC.md` §17. Three behaviours were **accepted** and
are stated publicly rather than removed:

- unique usernames reveal whether one is taken, so tries are rate-limited;
- a sender can see that a request is no longer pending;
- request timing was made uniform by sending mail after the response.

## Round two — at `dafb604`, after the fixes; fresh verifiers

The fresh verifiers had two duties: re-attack the changed code, and judge
every test a fixer had changed.

| Lens | Defects proven | Doors closed |
|---|---|---|
| privacy | 9 (2 medium) | 5 |
| identity | 6 (**1 high**) | 10 |
| abuse | 4 (low) | 15 |
| honesty | 11 (2 medium) | 7 |

**High: the pair lock failed in a production build.**

- **What happened.** `pairLock` checked `tx instanceof PgTransaction`. A
  production build bundles route handlers and pages with separate copies of
  drizzle-orm. After a health check or an export opened the database first,
  every pair-locked write failed until restart: block, friend request, like
  and accepting an invite.
- **Why the tests missed it.** The suite loads one copy, so it could not
  see this. The verifier found it over HTTP against `next build && next
  start`, and proved it in the suite with drizzle's second (CommonJS) build.
- **The fix.** The transaction is recognized by shape. The re-checker
  proved it in a production build, with the health request first and then
  the export first. As a control, the old check was put back and the same
  procedure failed.

**On the adapted tests,** the round-two verifiers found most changes sound:

- the re-staged race harnesses still failed on the old code, run by run;
- one rewrite had weakened a check. It asserted only that the founder
  script's source mentioned `invitedBy: null`, while the script could make
  any number of uninvited administrators. That became a round-two defect
  and was fixed: the script now makes the first account only.
