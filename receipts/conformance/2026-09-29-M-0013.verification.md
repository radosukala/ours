### Honesty

1. **HIGH, fixed.** The seat line, "…and you'll get the next one.", was
   untrue whenever anyone was already waiting: opening seats invites the
   longest-waiting address first. It now says the address joins the line
   and seats go to whoever has waited longest. The test walks the path:
   Ida waits, Zoe reads the line, one seat opens, Ida gets it.
2. **MEDIUM, fixed.** "After 100,000, a body of its members holds it, and
   can replace me." was a sentence about the handover not listed by exact
   text, and said more than the contract. It now names the domain, the
   data and the right to replace the maintainer, and is listed.
3. **MEDIUM, fixed in part.** The widened rule saw only "hand(s|ing) it
   to". It now sees the past tense and our.one by name, and the done-rule
   sees "the handover happened". **Accepted:** "It was Handed to its
   members at 100,000." still passes, because the status line is listed by
   exact text on every page; a whole-sentence allowlist is a later change
   to the scan.
4. **LOW, fixed.** "Have an invite? Open the link you were sent." showed
   only while joining was closed, when an invite does not work either. It
   now says the invite will work when joining opens.
5. **LOW, fixed.** The second 7% sentence rests on page 9 as well as
   page 8. The source now says "pages 8 and 9".
6. **LOW, fixed.** `front-page.test.ts` had grown weaker in two places
   (the "%" check read only visible text; the section checks confirmed
   sentences rather than whole sections). Both are restored there, and the
   verifier's own closed tests pin the whole page's text in six states.

What held: in every state the visible text is SPEC §18.15's, word for
word, with nothing extra; nothing states the handover as done or ownership
in the present tense; the listed sentences pass only on the front page;
"hand it to" is caught across line breaks, tags, entities and invisible
characters; the ten invites, the weekly email default, the join fields, the
lack of photos and the manifest are as the page says.

### Rendering

1. **MEDIUM, fixed.** The phone's caught-up marker was not the app's (a
   tick in a disc, no rules). The phone now renders the app's own
   `CaughtUpMarker`.
2. **MEDIUM, fixed.** The phone showed a state the app never draws: the
   caught-up marker with nothing older under it. The phone now carries a
   fourth, older post under the marker and ends on the app's `EndMarker`,
   the order `FeedList` draws for the same posts and last visit. The
   illustration's our.one side ends on the end marker's words.
3. **LOW, fixed.** The phone's top bar, post-row icons and tab colours now
   match the app: the wordmark, the four icons of `PostRow`, every tab in
   the text colour.
4. **LOW, fixed.** The card's byline and link were under 4.5:1 (dark
   byline 3.88:1; light link 2.84:1). Both are in the text colour now, and
   so are the three links under the promise. **Accepted:** the site's link
   blue (SPEC §9, 3.0:1 on white) still colours "Privacy", as on every
   page.
5. **LOW, fixed.** Lone words at narrow widths: the card's link no longer
   wraps, and the three links go one per line under 460px.
6. **LOW, fixed.** Lines of about 95 characters at 768px under "Keep your
   people": the column is capped at 34em.
7. **LOW, fixed.** Tap targets of 17 to 26px on a phone: 44px on touch
   screens.

What held: no sideways scroll or overlap from 300 to 1480px, in both
themes; the headline on two lines from 316px; the first phone screen shows
the headline, the lede, the form, the free-to-join line and the whole
card; all 24 states render with headings in order; exactly the decoration
is hidden from screen readers; every link has a name; in-page links land;
external links open in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`; the
focus ring is visible; every colour is a token with a dark value.

**Raised for the founder, not decided by agents:** the card hands "it",
all of our.one, where the contract names three things; and the footer's
