## Design direction

Adopted by the founder in `D-0001` and carried into the mandate's constraints.
The predecessor surface was near-black with condensed capitals at billboard
scale, acid green and orange, and a scarcity counter. It read as a token
launch, which contradicted `THESIS.md` §13 in the design while the document
disclaimed it in prose.

The trace is quiet instead: system UI sans for prose, system monospace for
identifiers, digests and states, near-monochrome, document structure.

Two notes on the execution:

- **No web font is loaded.** The mandate forbids third-party origins, and the
  system UI stack renders as the native interface font on each platform —
  which is closer to "the apps you use daily" than a downloaded Inter, at
  zero bytes and zero network. Reversible: self-hosting Inter is a font file
  and one line.
- **No framework.** ~380 lines produce the page with no runtime dependency.
  The repository has one dependency in total (`yaml`). This keeps the artifact
  digest trivially reproducible and the output rehostable by anyone, which is
  what makes the exit right more than a sentence. Reversible if a later
  mandate needs more.

## Two failures worth recording

**1. The articles table rendered its header and no rows.**

`parseArticles` matched headings beginning `R-`; the constitution's headings
read `## Article R-NAME — …`. Every article was silently dropped and the page
displayed an empty table under the promise "every article, and how it is
actually held".

This failed silently — an empty table still renders its heading, and the page
looked correct in a screenshot. Two guards added: the regex now tolerates the
prefix, and **the build refuses** if zero articles parse or if any article
carries no enforcement class.

**2. The prohibited-claim scan flagged the kernel's own honesty.**

`R-NO-FICTIONAL-OWNERSHIP` matched `non-bypassable` inside the kernel's
message *"so nothing here is non-bypassable"* — a denial of the prohibited
claim, caught by a regex that cannot read polarity.

This is the predicted false positive, and it is why the article is classed
`CHECKED` rather than `ENFORCED`. The wording changed rather than the rule,
and the replacement — "no gate here is beyond one person's reach" — is
plainer English than the double negative it replaced.

## A scope denial that held

The fix above also wanted applying to `packages/verifier/src/index.ts`, which
carries the same phrase. **`M-0001` does not allow `packages/verifier/**`.**

The verifier was left unchanged. Its text is not rendered into the page, so
no acceptance test required it, and widening scope for tidiness is the exact
habit the scope article exists to prevent. It can be changed under a mandate
that allows it.
