## Failures and corrections, in order

1. **A friendship through every seat.** Builders A and B both found that the
   unchanged join path would make every seat joiner the maintainer's friend.
   That would have given the maintainer the friends-only posts of everyone
   who came in through a seat. Seats now make no friendship and send no
   notification.
2. **A membership leak.** Builder B found that the public seat number moved
   for a stranger and stood still for a member. Every valid request now uses
   a seat.
3. **Promises the copy couldn't keep.** Examples: "nothing is kept unless
   you join", "$0.99" cited to a post that doesn't say it, and "delete it
   all" for suspended accounts. All corrected.
4. **The /join page promised seat joiners a friendship.** The architect
   found it in the browser; the tests and both builders had missed it.
5. **The fourth verification proved 25 defects** as failing tests: 2 HIGH, 10
   MEDIUM and 13 LOW.
   - Four were found by both verifiers.
   - One, the /join page, was already fixed by then.
   - All were fixed or recorded (SPEC §18.12 items 13–26).
6. **The re-check found 9 more,** 5 MEDIUM and 4 LOW, all fixed (SPEC §18.12
   item 27). One was a membership leak that the architect's own "line goes
   first" rule had created: the rule now applies to every request, so the
   address first in line learns nothing.
7. **The kernel refused scope twice.** Both times the cause was the
   verifiers' leftover worktree folders. They were removed after their
   branches were merged, and the check passed. The ENFORCED check did its
   job.

## Residual risk, recorded rather than fixed

1. **Someone with many addresses and client addresses can use up open
   seats.** Rate limits slow it down, and the administrator sees the count
   and can open more.
2. **A repeat request uses a seat without adding one.** That is the price of
   a public number that reveals nothing.
3. **A wave's emails run in one task after the response.** If it never
   runs, those addresses hold seats they were not told of; asking again
   sends a link.
4. **Nothing removes a join link's record automatically.** Retention is the
   founder's decision, as in M-0010.
5. **Every M-0010 residual still applies.**
