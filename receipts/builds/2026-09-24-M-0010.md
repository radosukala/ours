## What was built

`apps/web`: one core (`src/core`, plain TypeScript over Postgres, with no
framework imports) and a mobile-ready web application on it (Next.js
16.2.7, React 19.2.4).

**People and connections:**

- sign-in by an emailed link;
- accounts by invitation only, with 10 invites each;
- mutual friendship and one-way following;
- blocks and mutes;
- existing members confirm before an emailed invite connects them.

**Posts and the feed:**

- text posts to friends, or to friends and followers;
- one level of replies;
- likes that only the author sees;
- a feed in order that marks where you were caught up and ends at 14 days;
- in-app notifications;
- one weekly email that names who posted and never what.

**Safety and your data:**

- reporting, a moderation queue, statements of reasons, and a suspension
  notice;
- export and deletion.

**Public pages:**

- landing, rules, privacy;
- costs, rendered from `transparency/ledger.json`;
- who controls what, rendered from `transparency/control.json`;
- counts;
- a claims scan over every public string.

**The design follows the founder's note:** system fonts and the layout
people know from X and Instagram, with a bottom tab bar on phones, three
columns on desktop, and dark mode.

## Failures and corrections, in order

1. **Worktree base.** The parallel builders' git worktrees started at
   `origin/main` (`115728c`, before the foundation). Each builder noticed
   and fast-forwarded to local `main` before working. Later rounds were told
   to do so first.
2. **A cherry-pick with an invalid flag.** The architect's cherry-pick of
   the first verifiers' tests used an invalid flag. The script then deleted
   the branches. The commits were recovered by SHA, and nothing was lost.
3. **The kernel refused scope.** It refused because the builders' leftover
   worktree folders were untracked files outside the mandate's paths. The
   worktrees were removed (they were merged) and the check passed. The
   ENFORCED scope check did its job.
4. **Round one:** 53 failing tests from four verifiers.
   - **High:**
     - an emailed invite connected an existing member in one click;
     - `/privacy` named a supervisory authority no record states.
   - **Medium:** block races, list leaks, spoofable per-IP limits, gaps in
     the claims scan, and overstated rules.

   Decisions are in `SPEC.md` §17; three fixers closed them.
5. **Round two:** fresh verifiers.
   - **High:** `pairLock` used `instanceof PgTransaction`. A production
     build carries several copies of drizzle-orm, so once a route handler
     opened the database first, every pair-locked write (block, friend
     request, like, Add) failed until restart. The suite could not see it:
     vitest loads one copy.
   - **Medium:** four, including a claims scan that missed claims split by
     inline markup. Plus lows.
   - **Fixed.** The pair lock checks the transaction by shape, and the
     re-checker proved the fix in a production build.
6. **A session limit** stopped the final fixers once. The working tree was
   found clean, and the same workflow was re-run.
7. **The re-check** found a display-name bypass through invisible
   characters (soft hyphen, letter marks). It was fixed by refusing every
   default-ignorable and bidi-control character except the emoji variation
   selectors.

## Residual risk, recorded rather than fixed

1. **An old invite link shows its maker's current name.**
   - Someone the inviter later blocked, if they still hold one of the
     inviter's unused invite links, sees the inviter's current name and
     handle while signed out. Signed in, they are told only that the invite
     can't be used.
   - The remedy is to revoke the unused link. Tested as `recorded:` in
     `verify-privacy`.
2. **Usernames are unique, so trying one tells whether it is taken.**
   Changing your username is limited to 5 tries a day, and each join in
   progress to 5 tries. One invite allows 10 join links a day, so up to 50
   tries per invite per day. Stated on `/rules`.
3. **Per-address limits depend on `CLIENT_IP_HEADER`,** which a deployment
   must name. In production, sign-in and join requests are switched off
   until it is. In development the header is spoofable, as decided.
4. **A sender can see that a friend request is no longer pending.** Stated
   on `/rules`.
5. **Nothing expires automatically:** sign-in links, pending joins, email
   records, reports and the outbox. `/privacy` says so. Retention periods
   need a human decision.
6. **The two copies of drizzle-orm in a production build remain.** Any
   future class-identity check on a database object would fail the same
   way. The production-build check is DECLARED, not in CI.
7. **No test drives a page through a browser.** Page behaviour is tested
   through the core and server actions. Three builders and two verifiers
   drove pages by hand or over HTTP, and those results are recorded in their
   reports, not in the suite.
8. **The kernel's own claims check does not read `apps/web`.** `apps/web`
   has its own scan. Joining them is a kernel change, outside this mandate.
