## What this mandate is for

`M-0001` built a page that explains why it is running. `M-0006` builds the
gate that decides whether a community's tool may run at all: the records a
community's decisions and a tool's specification are written in, the
checks that hold them, the pin that stops an implementer changing the rules
it is judged by, and the denial suite that proves each refusal is for the
right reason. No runtime is built here. Nothing runs. The gate is proven on
a fictional community's records before any real community's exist.

3. `D-0006` and this mandate are `ADOPTED`.

## Objective

Build, in `spec/`, `communities/`, and the packages: the service
specification format `ours.tool/v0.1` — the meaning of a tool's records,
its behaviours as articles with reasons and enforcement classes, its
options with provenance, its contract with permitted disclosures, its
tests, and its transition requirements — with `ours.contract`,
`ours.charter`, `ours.standing`, `ours.vote`, and `ours.pin` record types
and a human-readable `spec/SPEC.md`; the kernel's ability to read a
community's root of records exactly as it reads the institution's; the
checks — a procedure check that a decision's recorded vote met its
charter's threshold and its actors held standing, a conformance mapping
that fails any article without a named test, a static contract check that
refuses an implementation reaching for a data class or a disclosure it did
not declare, and a pin check that refuses a specification drifted from its
approved digests or a contract widened or a transition requirement
weakened without an amending decision; CLI reporting in the same list as
the constitutional findings, with no combined total; a fictional community's
full specification, drafted for founder approval and pinned on approval;
and a denial suite in which every refusal is asserted by article and by
message.

## Human outcome

A founder — and later a community — can hand a builder a pinned
specification and know that the gate, not the builder's good conduct,
decides whether the implementation may run: an implementation that reaches
beyond its contract, changes the rules it is judged by, or runs under a
draft authority is refused, with the article named, in a sentence a person
can read.

## Adopted constraints

**The rules are records, and the implementer cannot write them**

- The specification, charter, standing count, contracts, and decisions of a
  community are repository records under `communities/<cell>/`. This
  mandate may draft the fixture's; the later implementation mandate is
  denied `communities/**` and `spec/**` entirely, and reads the pin.
- On founder approval, the fixture's specification, charter, and contracts
  are pinned: their digests recorded in `communities/dilna-fixture/PIN.yaml`.
  A change to any pinned file without a decision that names the amendment
  is a refusal, not a diff.
- The kernel treats any root of records the same; no code path is special
  to OURS's own records.

**Refusals are asserted, not assumed**

- Every check added here has at least one test asserting the article named
  and the message, not merely `authorized === false`.
- The static contract check needs no general program analysis: an
  application's only data access is the layer client, and the check
  enumerates the client's calls and their declared classes. If that
  hypothesis fails for the fixture, the mandate stops and the receipt says
  so.

**Bootstrap truth**

- Nothing runs, nothing is deployed, no environment is named, no domain is
  touched, no name is registered, and no real person's data exists. The
  fixture community is fictional and labelled so in every file and receipt.
- No fact marked `[CONFIRM]` is resolved. No controller or holder is named.

**Not a language**

- `ours.tool/v0.1` describes records, articles, options, contract, tests,
  and transition requirements. If it starts to acquire control flow,
  expressions, or a type system, the work stops and the receipt says so.

## Hypotheses

1. A data contract can be checked statically for an agent-generated
   TypeScript application whose only data access is the layer client.
2. A pin of digests is enough to hold a specification read-only against an
   implementer, without a second repository.
3. A fictional community's specification can be written in a day and
   approved in an hour.
4. The denial suite for community records needs no new kernel concepts
   beyond standing, vote, contract, and pin.

## Acceptance tests

1. **Authority, honestly.** `pnpm ours check M-0006` reports *authorised for
   execution* under a kernel that refuses a `DRAFT` record in the chain;
   if the check does not yet exist, the receipt says the result was *valid
   as a draft* and the work did not start.
2. The kernel reads `communities/dilna-fixture/` as a root and validates
   its chain; every invalid chain in Part D refuses for the correct reason,
   asserted by article and message.
3. The static contract check refuses an application that reaches for an
   undeclared data class, naming the class and the article; and refuses a
   disclosure of an undeclared field to a permitted origin.
4. A change to a pinned file without an amending decision is refused; a
   contract widened or a transition requirement weakened without one is
   refused.
5. Every article in the fixture's specification maps to a named test, and
   an article with no test fails conformance.
6. The CLI prints the community findings in the same list as the
   constitutional ones, each with its class, and no combined total.
7. The fixture is labelled fictional in every file and every surface the
   CLI prints.
8. `pnpm typecheck` and `pnpm test` pass; `pnpm ours check M-0006 --changed`
   authorises the finished working tree.

## Evidence required

- a conformance report mapping every fixture article and every
  constitutional article to a check and its class;
- build receipts per part recording agent, model, scope touched,
  dependencies added with reasons, cost, and every failure — the first
  line of the first receipt stating what the authority check reported;
- the founder's approval of the fixture's specification, and the pin;
- a proposed runtime mandate, written in the final receipt for founder
  review.

## Stop conditions

- any prerequisite above does not hold;
- `ours.tool/v0.1` begins to become a language;
- the static contract check cannot be made deterministic for the fixture's
  client calls;
- a fixture value would have to be chosen without founder approval, or a
  pinned file changed without a decision;
- a required fact is marked `[CONFIRM]` or would name a controller or a
  holder;
- the work requires a path outside the allowed scope;
- an acceptance test would require weakening a constitutional article.

## Risk

`LOW` and `ADDITIVE`: new record types, new checks, a fictional
community's records, no runtime, nothing deployed, no person affected.
