## Why an OPERATE mandate

This is the first OURS activity that touches people. Availability, needs,
names, and who was introduced to whom are personal data the moment the
steward writes them down. A founding run outside the ring would be the
first thing OURS did to real people while bypassing the machine. So it
runs inside it: this mandate names what is collected, where it lives, for
how long, who holds it, that every participant consented and can leave with
their data, and what success and failure look like — before the first
invitation.

## Objective

Assist one real community, by hand wherever a hand suffices and never by
typing records that belong in the layer, through the founding of a shared
tool: describe the need and invite the affected people; evaluate existing
software and reusable components, adopting one if it fits; agree a short,
readable founding arrangement — purpose, participation, decisions, money,
privacy, continuity — through guided choices with consequences shown;
name a builder, an operator, a budget, and one bounded first outcome; make
one real improvement together with dissent preserved; and rehearse
replacing the operator. The tool itself is delivered under `M-0006`'s
layer once `D-0007` adopts the holder; until then the founding produces
records, not software.

## Human outcome

A group of people who share a recurring need say, unprompted:

> *We use this. We understand what it costs. We know how to change it. Its
> future does not depend entirely on the person who built it.*

— or the record says which of those four sentences they could not say, and
why.

## Budget and stop rule

Founder time: six hours a week for eight weeks, `ESTIMATED`, recorded
weekly as cost under `LIFECYCLE.md` §4. **Stop if exceeded by half.** No
money is spent under this mandate except the founder's own; nothing is
subsidised without a visible source.

## Success criteria — set before the first invitation

The numbers are hypotheses and the founder's to adjust before adoption,
not after:

- at least half of participants take part in the founding arrangement;
- one real tradeoff is decided with the group, as a proposal and a decision
  with dissent preserved;
- the group adopts, adapts, or commissions one tool, and coordination that
  produces little new code counts as success;
- at least three participants state, with a number, what they would pay or
  contribute to sustain it;
- someone besides the founder operates for two weeks;
- one change is reversed on request and receipted;
- the output is `O-0001`, the first observation record, closing this
  mandate's hypotheses and `M-0006`'s as `CONFIRMED`, `REFUTED`, or
  `UNTESTED`.

## Hypotheses

1. A real group will decide a founding arrangement in about forty minutes
   of guided choices.
2. The group prefers adopting an existing tool over commissioning one when
   the cost of each is shown.
3. Someone besides the founder will take operational responsibility.

## Acceptance tests

1. `pnpm ours check M-0005` authorises before the first invitation.
2. Every participant's consent is on record before their data is; the
   record names these terms.
3. The founding arrangement exists as the community's charter record and
   validates as a chain under the kernel's community root.
4. One tradeoff is decided with a vote record whose tally the kernel checks
   against the charter's threshold.
5. Weekly cost is recorded; the stop rule is applied if triggered.
6. `O-0001` exists and closes every hypothesis with one state.

## Evidence required

- the community's records under `communities/<cell>/`;
- weekly receipts under `receipts/operate/`;
- `O-0001`.

## Stop conditions

- the hour budget is exceeded by half;
- a participant's data would have to be held under terms they did not
  consent to;
- the founder is, in practice, the boss of the community rather than a
  member;
- a fact marked `[CONFIRM]` would have to be inferred;
- the first invitation would precede `D-0007`.

## Risk

`MEDIUM` and `REVERSIBLE`: personal data of real people is held by hand
under stated terms; deletion on request restores the prior state of the
record but not of memory. Affected: the community's participants.

## Rollback

Delete every participant's data on request or at retention end; retain the
community's public records as deliberate projections with private detail
removed; close the mandate with `O-0001` stating what was learned.

**Irreversible residue:** introductions made, decisions people took part
in, and anything a participant already read cannot be unmade; no personal
data persists after deletion.
