## 1. The sentence

> **OURS is a member-sovereign institution that turns legitimate human
> authority into verifiable software.**

Its technical core is an **institution compiler**:

```text
AUTHORITY → MANDATE → SOFTWARE
```

- Authority identifies who may decide, under which rights and procedure.
- A mandate translates the legitimate decision into precise, bounded,
  testable implementation authority.
- Replaceable agents produce software from that mandate.
- Enforcement and evidence prove that the deployed result stayed inside it.

The institution is the product. Software is an output the institution can
inspect, replace, reverse, or fork.

> **OURS is a layer that belongs to the people in it. Applications are open
> source and replaceable; the people, their data, and their rules are not.**

Added under `D-0006` on 8 September 2026 as working material beside the
sentence above — a candidate in the §15 register, not adopted copy. The
direction it names is [THE-LAYER.md](./THE-LAYER.md).

## 2. The problem is upstream of code

The familiar software-company sequence is:

```text
idea
→ company
→ capital
→ team
→ code
→ distribution
→ users
→ revenue pressure
→ optimization for whoever controls the company
```

The user receives a product but normally receives no authority over the system
that increasingly mediates work, relationships, identity, knowledge, money,
health, childhood, or public speech.

The product's consequential rules are fragmented across:

- a cap table and board authority;
- employment and vendor contracts;
- product requirements and private roadmaps;
- source code and infrastructure configuration;
- ranking objectives and training data;
- safety, moderation, and support processes;
- terms of service that the company may change;
- production credentials controlled by a small operator group.

The visible application is therefore not the real source. It is a compiled
consequence of the institution behind it.

Open-source code alone does not repair this. A user may inspect or fork code
while the company still controls the domain, trademark, user graph, production
data, treasury, legal entity, and distribution. A governance poll does not
repair it either if the operator may ignore the result or quietly deploy
something else.

The problem is an unbroken control chain from legitimate authority to actual
runtime behavior.

## 3. Why this becomes possible now

Software construction is becoming dramatically more abundant.

[Lovable reports](https://lovable.dev/motion-tutorial) 1.2 million projects
built on its platform and 60 million monthly visits to those projects. These
are company-reported cumulative figures. They are not a build rate, not an
independent measure of production quality, and not evidence that those projects
became maintained products — but they illustrate the direction. Non-developers
can describe and deploy working applications; coding agents can work across
entire repositories; open-source frameworks and managed infrastructure already
provide much of the underlying stack.

This does **not** mean safe products are free. Security, reliability, support,
moderation, privacy, legal compliance, incident response, insurance, payments,
and human judgment remain costly. The scarcity moves upstream from typing code
to deciding what should exist and proving that it behaves correctly.

The research framing in
[Cheap Code, Costly Judgment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01087) is consistent
with this thesis: abundant agentic implementation increases the importance of
inspectability, correction, maintainability, evidence, and governance.

At the same time, the legitimacy problem is visible to ordinary users.
[Pew Research Center found](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/10/18/views-of-data-privacy-risks-personal-data-and-digital-privacy-laws/)
that 81% of surveyed US adults were concerned about how companies use their
data, 73% felt they had little or no control over company-collected data, and
77% had little or no trust in social-media leaders to take responsibility for
data misuse. The figures are US-specific and from 2023; they establish a real
trust problem, not guaranteed demand for OURS.

People sometimes believe that an application is secretly listening through a
microphone because its targeting feels impossibly specific. OURS does not need
to validate that technical claim. The belief itself exposes the institutional
failure: users cannot inspect the objective, inputs, production behavior, or
accountability chain well enough to distinguish explanation from suspicion.

The opportunity is not a better privacy policy. It is software whose authority,
objectives, constraints, changes, and operating evidence are legible by design.

## 4. The company is already a compiler

A corporation is an institutional technology. Its charter, cap table, board,
contracts, incentives, and law determine who can authorize work and who receives
its economic benefit. Employees and suppliers translate those decisions into a
product.

In simplified form:

```text
CAPITAL AUTHORITY
→ board and executive mandates
→ employee implementation
→ software optimized for company objectives
```

OURS changes the source:

```text
MEMBER AUTHORITY
→ constitutional mandates
→ agent and steward implementation
→ software constrained by member rights
```

This does not require pretending that corporations, expertise, capital, or
professional operations disappear. They become bounded instruments of the
institution rather than its automatic sovereign.

- A corporation may employ builders and sign contracts.
- A steward may make fast operational decisions inside a delegated mandate.
- An expert body may block unsafe action within a defined jurisdiction.
- Capital may receive an explicit, limited contractual return.
- None of those roles silently gains unlimited constitutional authority.

Profit remains necessary for some products, but its position changes:

> **Profit may be a liveness constraint. It is not automatically the supreme
> objective function.**

## 5. The category

An institution compiler is not a natural-language app builder and not a voting
interface attached to a coding agent.

It is the complete system that maintains this attributable chain:

```text
human-readable constitution
→ legitimate proposal and deliberation
→ authorized decision
→ typed implementation mandate
→ bounded agent work
→ constitutional and product verification
→ separately authorized deployment
→ public release evidence
→ observed outcomes
→ appeal, correction, or reversal
→ enforceable ownership and exit
```

Every link matters. Removing one produces a familiar but incomplete product:

| Missing link | What remains |
|---|---|
| legitimate authority | an AI builder with a policy file |
| precise mandate | ambiguous voting followed by improvisation |
| enforcement | a constitution the operator may ignore |
| deployment proof | public source code with unverifiable production |
| legal and asset control | simulated ownership |
| operations and appeals | governance that works only when nothing goes wrong |
| exit | a benevolent platform that can later enclose its users |

The potential invention is not any individual primitive. It is making the
whole chain one inspectable and non-bypassable product.

## 6. Human authority and machine execution are separate layers

“The pact is the coding language” collapses legitimacy, specification, and
implementation.

A constitution may say “treat members fairly.” It cannot by itself determine:

- what fairness means in a particular ranking decision;
- which trade-offs are permitted;
- what data may be used;
- which exceptional cases require discretion;
- what evidence is sufficient;
- who interprets a conflict between two rights.

The correct separation is:

1. **Authority:** who has standing to decide and what higher rules bind them.
2. **Mandate:** the typed, testable, versioned meaning of the authorized change.
3. **Implementation:** one replaceable way to satisfy the mandate.

Human-readable constitutional language remains authoritative. Machine-readable
rules are linked projections, not silent replacements. This follows an
important principle in current
[OECD Law as Code work](https://www.oecd.org/en/events/public-consultations/2026/07/consultation-on-the-digital-provision-of-law-towards-a-shared-reference-framework-for-law-as-code.html):
the authoritative text remains binding, executable representations remain
linked to it, and interpretive or discretionary elements stay visible rather
than being falsely converted into deterministic rules.

Agents may draft an interpretation. They may not decide that their
interpretation is legitimate.

## 7. What member sovereignty means

“User-owned” is not a marketing adjective. It must be falsifiable.

An institution is member-sovereign only when its rules and legal arrangements
answer yes to questions such as:

- Can the appropriate members remove or replace the people operating it?
- Can a deploy that violates the constitution be stopped?
- Can members inspect who authorized a consequential change?
- Are emergency powers narrow, expiring, reviewable, and reversible?
- Does member authority reach the legal entity, domain, trademark, repository,
  infrastructure, treasury, and production data?
- Can members obtain their data and relationship graph in usable form?
- Can the institution or a legitimate subset fork without the founder's
  permission?
- Can the founder change member rights unilaterally after the transfer gate?

If the founder or a conventional company indefinitely controls all operational
keys and assets, member ownership is a user interface.

Legal membership, beneficial economics, constitutional voting, product use,
and early participation are different things. No click, referral, follower,
purchase, contribution score, or amount of attention should silently create
extra constitutional votes.

## 8. One institution, many Product Cells

A million people cannot govern every button in every product. OURS therefore
needs a federal architecture.

The root institution holds:

- membership and identity rules;
- universal rights and prohibitions;
- constitutional amendment;
- common asset and treasury obligations;
- due process, appeal, audit, portability, and exit;
- the rules for chartering and dissolving Product Cells.

A **Product Cell** is a bounded institution for one product or mission. It has:

- a defined constituency;
- a charter and purpose;
- affected-person standing;
- delegated stewards and operators;
- local budgets, proposals, and mandates;
- product-specific safety and expertise;
- releases and outcome evidence;
- an exit relationship to the root institution.

Members govern the Cells they use, fund, operate, or are materially affected
by according to rules defined in advance. The root does not become a global
feature-voting forum.

This is particularly important for products affecting children, health,
finance, employment, or public speech. Parents, children, professionals,
operators, and the wider public may have different standing. Majority rule
cannot be allowed to erase the rights of a child or another protected minority.

## 9. Algorithms become constitutional objects

The goal is not to promise that every internal weight of every learned model is
intuitively understandable. That promise would often be false.

The institution can make the governing contract understandable:

- the objective and prohibited objectives;
- allowed and forbidden input classes;
- whether payment may affect ranking;
- member-selectable modes, including a non-personalized option;
- the explanation available for an individual outcome;
- model, policy, and data-version provenance;
- evaluation metrics, error rates, and measured harms;
- change authority and notice periods;
- appeal, audit, shutdown, and rollback procedures.

Current regulation already moves partway in this direction. The EU Digital
Services Act requires greater transparency about recommender-system parameters
and gives users of very large platforms an option not based on profiling, but
this remains different from users holding constitutional authority over the
system. [European Commission overview](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/dsa-impact-platforms)

OURS must offer more than description. It must connect the declared algorithm
contract to the production version and to continuing outcome evidence.

## 10. Economics without surveillance sovereignty

The new thesis does not promise that every product is free or that every member
receives a dividend.

It adopts these economic directions:

- no sale of behavioral data;
- no hidden advertising auction as the default payer;
- no pay-to-govern or wealth-weighted constitutional authority;
- transparent product costs and treasury flows;
- payment for real labor and professional stewardship;
- explicit, capped, reviewable terms for external capital;
- pricing aligned with the service members choose to sustain;
- no economic right invented before legal, tax, securities, and cooperative
  review.

Possible revenue sources include member subscriptions, transparent service or
transaction fees, institutional contracts, voluntary contributions, and
purpose-compatible capital. The appropriate mix belongs to each Product Cell.

The test is not whether OURS avoids revenue. It is whether the payer, objective,
authority, and beneficiary can be stated without contradiction.

## 11. Defensibility, and the definition of the product

Code cannot be the primary moat if code can be generated and forked.

OURS compounds a different set of assets:

- a legitimate and active member institution;
- accumulated trust earned through constraint and truthful receipts;
- constitutional history and institutional memory;
- a graph of real relationships and commitments that people can carry;
- proven decision and operating processes;
- credible stewardship and safety capability;
- interoperable Product Cells;
- the continuing choice of members to recognize one canonical institution.

Forkability is not the absence of defensibility. It disciplines the canonical
institution to remain worth choosing.

Under `D-0006` (8 September 2026) the assets this section lists are no
longer only what defends OURS; they are what OURS *holds* — identity,
standing, data, relationships, law, records, money, and names, in trust for
the people in it — and an application is a replaceable tenant on that
ground, with a contract for what it may reach. [THE-LAYER.md](./THE-LAYER.md)
is the direction; the running instance is preferred for exactly as long as
it deserves to be.

## 12. Prior art and the honest novelty claim

Important pieces already exist:

- [PolicyKit](https://policykit.org/) lets online communities author and execute
  governance policies.
- [Gov4Git](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/gov4git-a-decentralized-platform-for-community-governance/)
  connects configurable community governance with git-based collaboration.
- [Decidim](https://docs.decidim.org/en/develop/understand/social-contract)
  provides traceable participatory governance under a strong social contract.
- [Open Policy Agent](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs) provides general
  policy-as-code enforcement.
- [Solid](https://solidproject.org/about) advances user-controlled data and
  application portability.
- DAOs connect transparent rules, proposals, treasuries, and automatic
  execution, often with token-based authority.
- Platform cooperatives pursue democratic ownership of digital platforms.
- Emerging systems such as [Senordo](https://senordo.com/) and the
  [DAP Compiler](https://dap.solutions/) use compiler language for governed
  enterprise systems and executable institutional policy.
- [Solid](https://solidproject.org/) — personal data pods with
  application-scoped access.
- The [AT Protocol](https://atproto.com/)'s personal data servers and
  portable identity.
- [Matrix](https://matrix.org/) and [Nostr](https://nostr.com/) — open
  protocols in which the network, not one operator, carries the graph.
- [Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/) — fiscal hosting for
  groups without a legal entity of their own.
- GitHub's [spec-kit](https://github.com/github/spec-kit) — specification-
  driven development, in which the specification is the primary artifact
  and code its expression.

*The five entries above were added under `D-0006` on 8 September 2026 from
the agent's reading. Confirmed by the founder the same day, in conversation,
as a blanket confirmation of the list rather than link by link — recorded
as such.*

OURS therefore must not claim to have invented governance, policy engines,
open source, cooperatives, software receipts, or AI coding.

The novelty hypothesis is narrower and stronger:

> **No mature general product identified in the August 2026 review makes
> member legitimacy, precise authority, AI implementation, constitutional
> conformance, actual deployment, legal asset control, and usable exit one
> end-to-end attributable object.**

This remains a competitive-research hypothesis, not a fact proven by a finite
search.

Beside the data-layer prior art, the same kind of hypothesis, under
`D-0006`: none of those systems makes *standing* (who decides), *admission
by the users' constitution*, and *holding in trust* one object with the
data layer.

## 13. What OURS is not

OURS is not:

- a token, blockchain, or speculative ownership scheme;
- a one-million-person waitlist;
- a social network with feature polls;
- “vibe coding by democracy”;
- a no-code product generator;
- an open-source license presented as user ownership;
- a platform cooperative with no executable connection to production;
- a constitution stored beside code but bypassable by operators;
- a claim that teams, capital, security, support, or judgment are unnecessary;
- a universal referendum on every operational decision;
- a promise that people will join merely because incumbent platforms are
  distrusted.

## 14. Falsification and failure conditions

OURS should be treated as disproven or in need of fundamental revision if the
project cannot demonstrate that:

1. a third party can trace a running release to legitimate authority;
2. an unauthorized or out-of-scope agent change is reliably blocked;
3. the human institution can resolve ambiguity without hiding the judgment;
4. a steward can operate quickly inside a mandate without constant voting;
5. members understand and use consequential rights rather than merely praising
   the idea;
6. the institution can pay for safe operation without recreating surveillance
   incentives;
7. legal and asset control can be made consistent with the public constitution;
8. a product can be exported or forked without founder permission;
9. governance resists low participation, capture, harassment, Sybil attacks,
   emergency abuse, and majority harm;
10. at least one Product Cell produces personal utility strong enough that
    people adopt it without being paid to perform governance.

The project should measure behavior, not declarations of support. Distrust of
incumbents is not retention. A vote is not a customer. Virality is not
legitimacy.

## 15. The brand promise

The brand remains simply **OURS** because it names the governing relationship,
not one implementation mechanism.

The public language should eventually be shorter than this thesis. Candidate
lines are working material, not adopted copy:

> **PEOPLE DECIDE. AGENTS BUILD. RULES HOLD.**

> **THE SOFTWARE WE LIVE IN SHOULD BE OURS.**

> **SEE THE RULES. CHANGE THE RULES. OWN THE RESULT.**

The proof must arrive before the superlative. The public should encounter a
working release trace, a comprehensible algorithm contract, and a real exit
before being asked to believe that OURS can build anything.

## 16. The thesis in full

AI is making implementation abundant while software institutions remain
capital-sovereign, opaque, and difficult to leave. OURS proposes that the
people governed by software can instead become the constitutional source of
authority behind it. Human decisions are translated into precise mandates;
replaceable agents implement those mandates; tests and policy gates constrain
the result; deployment receipts connect the running artifact to its authority;
professional stewards operate within delegated bounds; and legal ownership,
assets, data, appeal, and exit make the authority real.

If this works, OURS is not merely another user-owned application. It is a way
to make user-owned applications buildable, governable, and operationally
competitive in the AI coding era.
